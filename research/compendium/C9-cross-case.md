### Theory and maritime security

- **Bueger, C. and Edmunds, T. (2017). Beyond seablindness: a new agenda for maritime security studies.** International Affairs 93(6). [link](https://academic.oup.com/ia/article/93/6/1293/4111108) — **Grade A1**. Summary: Argues that politicians and scholars ignored the sea and sets out a research agenda built on lessons from counter-piracy, such as information sharing and capacity-building. It gives you the key vocabulary for your literature review. Reliability: A top, highly cited international relations journal, but it is theory rather than data.
- **Bueger, C. (2015). What is maritime security?** Marine Policy 53. [link](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.marpol.2014.12.005) — **Grade A1**. Summary: Shows that "maritime security" has no agreed meaning, which lets many actors work together without agreeing on everything but can hide political disagreement. Useful for your definitions section. Reliability: One of the most cited papers in the field, free to read.
- **Bueger, C. and Edmunds, T. (2024). Understanding Maritime Security.** Oxford University Press. [link](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/understanding-maritime-security/) — **Grade A2**. Summary: A short textbook that sorts sea threats into state conflict, terrorism and "blue crime" such as piracy, and explains how states cooperate against each. That threat-type split helps explain why cooperation worked against Malacca piracy but struggles against Iran or the Houthis. Reliability: Leading scholars with a top academic publisher.
- **Till, G. (2026). Seapower: A Guide for the Twenty-First Century (5th edn).** Routledge. [link](https://rsis.edu.sg/staff-publication/seapower-a-guide-for-the-twenty-first-century/) — **Grade A2**. Summary: The standard seapower textbook, updated for Ukraine, Gaza and the Indo-Pacific. It covers good order at sea and naval cooperation. Reliability: Written by a senior King's College London professor, though page counts differ between listings.
- **Bueger, C. (2013). Practice, pirates and coast guards: the grand narrative of Somali piracy.** Third World Quarterly 34(10). [link](https://doi.org/10.1080/01436597.2013.851896) — **Grade A2**. Summary: Uses pirates' own interviews to show they justified piracy as "coast guarding" against foreign fishing and dumping. This helps explain why piracy lasted despite naval patrols. Reliability: A peer-reviewed qualitative study, free to read.
- **Shortland, A. and Vothknecht, M. (2011). Combating "maritime terrorism" off the coast of Somalia.** European Journal of Political Economy 27(S1). [link](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejpoleco.2011.03.004) — **Grade A2**. Summary: Judges the 2008–10 naval mission off Somalia, including EU Atalanta, a partial success, because attacks were pushed out of the guarded corridor into open ocean. A key counter-argument: cooperation can move a threat rather than remove it. Reliability: Peer-reviewed and mixes statistics with interviews.
- **Percy, S. and Shortland, A. (2013). The business of piracy in Somalia.** Journal of Strategic Studies 36(4). [link](https://doi.org/10.1080/01402390.2012.750242) — **Grade A2**. Summary: Argues that Somali piracy is a business run from land, so naval patrols alone cannot end it. A useful limit on the "cooperation works" argument. Reliability: Peer-reviewed in a leading strategic-studies journal.
- **Bensassi, S. and Martínez-Zarzoso, I. (2012). How costly is modern maritime piracy to the international community?** Review of International Economics 20(5). [link](https://doi.org/10.1111/roie.12000) — **Grade A2**. Summary: A trade model of Europe–Asia trade finds that hijackings cut exports by making deliveries less reliable, costing about US$24.5 billion a year. It shows piracy, not just war, has measurable trade costs. Reliability: Peer-reviewed, with a Spanish co-author and a free repository copy.
- **[Author to check] (2015). Learning from piracy: future challenges of maritime security governance.** Global Affairs. [link](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23340460.2015.960170) — **Grade A3**. Summary: Draws lessons from Somali counter-piracy for how sea security should be run. It highlights loose, informal groups like the Contact Group on Piracy as a model. Reliability: Peer-reviewed, but the author is unconfirmed and only snippets were seen.
- **[Author to check] (2013). Communities of security practice at work? The emerging African maritime security regime.** African Security. [link](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19392206.2013.853579) — **Grade A3**. Summary: Argues that the fight against piracy sharply increased maritime cooperation and created early "security communities". Good for the theory of why cooperation forms. Reliability: Peer-reviewed, but the author is unknown and it focuses on Africa.
- **[Author to check] (2019). Piracy studies coming of age: a window on the making of maritime intervention actors.** International Affairs 95(5). [link](https://academic.oup.com/ia/article/95/5/1037/5537420) — **Grade A3**. Summary: A review of piracy research showing how states and coalitions become "maritime intervention actors". A good anchor for your literature review. Reliability: A top journal, but the author still needs checking.
- **[Author to check] (2009). Coalition strategy and the pirates of the Gulf of Aden and the Red Sea.** Comparative Strategy. [link](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01495930903025250) — **Grade A3**. Summary: Analyses how naval coalitions were organised against Somali piracy. An early comparison for later coalitions at the same chokepoint, and for coalition theory generally. Reliability: Peer-reviewed but US-centred and written before outcomes were known.
- **[Authors to check] (2025). The economic impact of piracy: a critical assessment of maritime security and trade disruptions in the Gulf of Guinea.** Maritime Economics & Logistics. [link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41278-025-00344-1) — **Grade A3**. Summary: Uses realism and critical theory to explain why responses to piracy are often fragmented and go-it-alone. A useful counter-example where cooperation was weak. Reliability: A leading niche journal, but authors unconfirmed and outside your three cases.
- **[Authors to check] (n.d.). Shipping network disruption, vulnerability and resilience amidst geopolitical tensions.** Maritime Business Review 10(3). [link](https://www.emerald.com/mabr/article/10/3/260/1270557/Shipping-network-disruption-vulnerability-and) — **Grade A6**. Summary: Studies how vulnerable shipping networks are, and how they recover, when politics disrupts them. It may support your theory of chokepoint vulnerability and resilience. Reliability: Peer-reviewed, but only the title was seen, so read the abstract first.
- **[Authors to check] (2026). Come sail your ships around me: wartime trade, distance, and the impact of the global shipping industry on the pacifying effect of trade.** Defence and Peace Economics. [link](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10242694.2026.2665844) — **Grade A6**. Summary: Asks how global shipping and rerouting change the idea that trade keeps the peace. Possible theory support. Reliability: A respected journal, but only the title was seen.
- **[Authors to check] (2023). Assessment of global shipping risk caused by maritime piracy.** Heliyon. [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405844023081963) — **Grade B2**. Summary: Maps piracy risk worldwide and suggests that anti-piracy measures in one area pushed piracy elsewhere rather than ending it. Reliability: A large open-access journal with lighter review, and the displacement point comes from one snippet.
- **Chalk, P. (2008). The Maritime Dimension of International Security: Terrorism, Piracy, and Challenges for the United States.** RAND Corporation (MG-697). [link](https://library.mumbai.bristol.ac.uk/Record/doab-20.500.12854ir-114734) — **Grade B2**. Summary: Explains why piracy and sea terrorism rose after 2000, pointing to weak port security, poor coastal surveillance and busy chokepoints. It also reviews US-led security initiatives. Reliability: A free, well-regarded report, but RAND is US-government-linked.
- **Fishman, E. (2025). Chokepoints: American Power in the Age of Economic Warfare.** Portfolio/Penguin. [link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chokepoints) — **Grade C2**. Summary: About economic chokepoints (finance, technology, energy) used in sanctions on Iran, Russia and China, not mainly sea straits. Use it only for short context on why states use chokepoints as leverage. Reliability: A well-reviewed popular book by a former US sanctions official, so cite the publisher's page, not Wikipedia.

### Risk methodology (the equation)

- **ISO (2018). ISO 31000:2018 Risk management — Guidelines.** International Organization for Standardization. [link](https://www.iso.org/standard/65694.html) — **Grade A1**. Summary: The international standard that defines risk as the "effect of uncertainty on objectives", described through likelihood and consequences. It justifies "Risk = Likelihood × Severity". Reliability: The official global standard, though it says nothing about cooperation.
- **IEC (2019). IEC 31010:2019 Risk management — Risk assessment techniques (2nd edn).** International Electrotechnical Commission. [link](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=IEC+31010%3A2019+Risk+assessment+techniques) — **Grade A1**. Summary: The companion catalogue to ISO 31000, listing assessment methods including the likelihood/consequence matrix. It says the right method depends on purpose and data. Reliability: An official standard; the link is a search because no URL was recorded.
- **Kaplan, S. and Garrick, B.J. (1981). On the quantitative definition of risk.** Risk Analysis 1(1). [link](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Kaplan+Garrick+1981+On+the+quantitative+definition+of+risk) — **Grade A1**. Summary: The classic paper defining risk as three questions: what can go wrong, how likely is it, and what are the consequences. It underpins every formula in your equation. Reliability: A foundational, very widely cited peer-reviewed paper; check the page range.
- **Cox, L.A. (2008a). What's wrong with risk matrices?** Risk Analysis 28(2). [link](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6924.2008.01030.x) — **Grade A1**. Summary: Shows that risk matrices give very different risks the same score and that users can rate the same risk in opposite ways. A key warning to state in your evaluation. Reliability: A highly cited peer-reviewed paper in the leading risk journal.
- **National Research Council (2010). Review of the Department of Homeland Security's Approach to Risk Analysis.** National Academies Press. [link](https://doi.org/10.17226/12972) — **Grade A1**. Summary: Found splitting risk into threat, vulnerability and consequence "reasonable", but said simply multiplying them fails for terrorism because a clever enemy adapts. Relevant to adaptive threats like the Houthis. Reliability: An expert review by the US National Academies.
- **Marin-Ferrer, M., Vernaccini, L. and Poljansek, K. (2017). Index for Risk Management INFORM: Concept and Methodology, Version 2017.** EU Publications Office (EUR 28655 EN). [link](https://doi.org/10.2760/094023) — **Grade A1**. Summary: Explains the INFORM index, which combines hazard, vulnerability and "lack of coping capacity" with a geometric mean. It is the model for your cooperation-adjusted equation. Reliability: The official methodology of the EU Joint Research Centre.
- **IPCC (2014). Summary for policymakers, Climate Change 2014: Impacts, Adaptation, and Vulnerability.** Cambridge University Press. [link](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar5/wg2/) — **Grade A1**. Summary: Defines risk as hazards interacting with the exposure and vulnerability of systems, including lack of capacity to cope. Supports treating cooperation as coping capacity. Reliability: An authoritative, heavily reviewed UN scientific assessment.
- **UNGA (2016). Report of the open-ended intergovernmental expert working group on indicators and terminology relating to disaster risk reduction (A/71/644).** United Nations. [link](https://undocs.org/A/71/644) — **Grade A1**. Summary: The UN's official disaster-risk terms, treating risk as a function of hazard, exposure, vulnerability and capacity. Supports giving capacity its own place in the equation. Reliability: An official UN document agreed by governments.
- **IMO (2002). Guidelines for Formal Safety Assessment (FSA) for use in the IMO rule-making process (MSC/Circ.1023).** International Maritime Organization. [link](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=IMO+MSC%2FCirc.1023+Formal+Safety+Assessment+guidelines) — **Grade A1**. Summary: The original shipping risk method, using a log-scaled matrix where Risk Index = Frequency Index + Severity Index. Shows that adding log scores equals multiplying. Reliability: A primary IMO document, though the matrix detail was taken from secondary sources.
- **IMO (2018). Revised Guidelines for Formal Safety Assessment (MSC-MEPC.2/Circ.12/Rev.2).** International Maritime Organization. [link](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=MSC-MEPC.2%2FCirc.12%2FRev.2+Formal+Safety+Assessment) — **Grade A1**. Summary: The current IMO risk guidelines, defining risk as frequency combined with severity and requiring incident data to be checked for reliability. Reliability: A primary document from the UN shipping agency; the link is a search.
- **OECD/JRC (Nardo, M. et al.) (2008). Handbook on Constructing Composite Indicators: Methodology and User Guide.** OECD Publishing. [link](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Handbook+on+Constructing+Composite+Indicators+OECD+2008) — **Grade A1**. Summary: The standard guide to building an index step by step: choosing data, normalising, weighting, combining and testing sensitivity. It guides how you build and test your score. Reliability: An official OECD and EU handbook, widely used.
- **Saisana, M., Saltelli, A. and Tarantola, S. (2005). Uncertainty and sensitivity analysis techniques as tools for the quality assessment of composite indicators.** Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series A 168(2). [link](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-985X.2005.00350.x) — **Grade A1**. Summary: Shows how to test whether an index's rankings survive changes in weights and methods. Backs up your sensitivity test. Reliability: A peer-reviewed paper in a top statistics journal.
- **Cox, L.A. (2008b). Some limitations of "Risk = Threat × Vulnerability × Consequence" for risk analysis of terrorist attacks.** Risk Analysis 28(6). [link](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Cox+2008+limitations+Risk+%3D+Threat+%C3%97+Vulnerability+%C3%97+Consequence) — **Grade A2**. Summary: Lists four problems with multiplying threat, vulnerability and consequence, including ignoring links between them and subjective scores. Reliability: Peer-reviewed in the leading risk journal; details seen only in search results.
- **Willis, H.H. (2007). Guiding resource allocations based on terrorism risk.** Risk Analysis 27(3). [link](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6924.2007.00909.x) — **Grade A2**. Summary: Extends terrorism risk scores to decisions about where to spend defence money and how much risk to accept. Reliability: Peer-reviewed, but written for US homeland security rather than shipping.
- **Pristrom, S., Yang, Z., Wang, J. and Yan, X. (2016). A novel flexible model for piracy and robbery assessment of merchant ship operations.** Reliability Engineering & System Safety 155. [link](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Pristrom+2016+novel+flexible+model+for+piracy+and+robbery+assessment) — **Grade A2**. Summary: A probability model of a ship being hijacked off East Africa, including the security measures in place. It shows cooperation and protection act on likelihood. Reliability: Peer-reviewed engineering journal, but it needs ship-level data beyond an EPQ.
- **CISA (n.d.). Risk Analysis and Management for Critical Asset Protection (RAMCAP).** US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. [link](https://www.cisa.gov/mts-resilience-resources/risk-analysis-and-management-critical-asset-protection-ramcap) — **Grade A2**. Summary: The US infrastructure risk method that multiplies threat, vulnerability and consequence. It is the source of the T × V × C formula. Reliability: An official US government page, though the formula detail is partly confirmed via other sources.
- **JRC DRMKC (n.d.). INFORM Risk: Methodology.** European Commission. [link](https://drmkc.jrc.ec.europa.eu/inform-index/INFORM-Risk/Methodology) — **Grade A2**. Summary: The web summary of INFORM's three dimensions and how they are combined. A quick companion to Marin-Ferrer et al. (2017). Reliability: An official EU page, undated, so cite the 2017 report for detail.
- **JRC COIN (2023). Step 6: Aggregation [training slides].** European Commission. [link](https://knowledge4policy.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/COIN_2023_Step%206.%20Aggregation.pdf) — **Grade A2**. Summary: Explains that an average lets one strong score cancel a weak one, while a geometric mean punishes unbalanced profiles. Supports your choice of geometric mean. Reliability: Official EU training material, not a formal publication.
- **George, A.L. and Bennett, A. (2005). Case Studies and Theory Development in the Social Sciences.** MIT Press. [link](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=George+Bennett+2005+Case+Studies+and+Theory+Development+in+the+Social+Sciences) — **Grade A2**. Summary: The standard guide to comparing case studies and to process tracing, which you use to separate the effect of cooperation from other causes. Reliability: A leading academic methods book, carried over from an earlier file without a fresh check.
- **Fan, H., Lu, J., Chang, Z. and Ji, Y. (2024). [Bayesian network-based TOPSIS framework for dynamic control of maritime piracy risk].** Maritime Policy & Management 51(7). [link](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/marpmg/v51y2024i7p1582-1601.html) — **Grade A3**. Summary: Links a data-driven probability model to a ranking method to compare anti-piracy strategies by how much they cut likelihood. Shows the advanced methods you chose not to use. Reliability: Peer-reviewed, but the exact title still needs copying from RePEc.
- **Willis, H.H., Morral, A.R., Kelly, T.K. and Medby, J.J. (2005). Estimating Terrorism Risk (MG-388).** RAND Corporation. [link](https://doi.org/10.7249/MG388) — **Grade B1**. Summary: Written for US homeland security funding, it recommends defining terrorism risk as expected yearly consequences. A source for the threat-vulnerability-consequence family of formulas. Reliability: A respected, widely cited think-tank study, though US-government-funded.
- **BIMCO, ICS, IGP&I Clubs, INTERTANKO and OCIMF (2018). BMP5: Best Management Practices to Deter Piracy and Enhance Maritime Security.** Witherby Publishing Group. [link](https://wwwcdn.imo.org/localresources/en/OurWork/Security/Documents/BMP5%20small.pdf) — **Grade B1**. Summary: The shipping industry's guide for the Red Sea, Gulf of Aden and Indian Ocean, requiring a threat assessment, then a ship risk assessment, then protective measures. Its order matches threat × vulnerability. Reliability: Primary industry guidance hosted by the IMO, though written by shipping bodies.
- **Arslanalp, S., Koepke, R. and Verschuur, J. (2021). Tracking Trade from Space: An Application to Pacific Island Countries (WP/21/225).** International Monetary Fund. [link](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Arslanalp+Koepke+Verschuur+2021+Tracking+Trade+from+Space) — **Grade B1**. Summary: Explains how ship-tracking satellite data is turned into trade estimates. It is the method behind PortWatch, so it tells you what those numbers can and cannot show. Reliability: An IMF working paper, expert but not peer-reviewed.
- **ADRC (n.d.). Chapter 2, Section 2.1 [disaster risk].** Asian Disaster Reduction Center. [link](https://www.adrc.asia/publications/LWR/LWR_pdf/Chapter%202%20Section%202_1.pdf) — **Grade B2**. Summary: A teaching version of the formula Risk = Hazard × Vulnerability ÷ Capacity, which puts capacity below the line. Supports treating cooperation as capacity. Reliability: An intergovernmental centre, but undated teaching material.
- **Argus Media (n.d.). Explainer: war risk insurance and AWRP.** Argus Media. [link](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2795769-explainer-war-risk-insurance-and-awrp) — **Grade B2**. Summary: Explains that extra war-risk premiums are quoted as a percentage of a ship's hull value and are required in "listed areas". Helps you use insurance as a likelihood measure. Reliability: A major commodity-pricing agency, but undated and only seen in search results.

### International law

- **United Nations (1982). UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), Part III, Articles 34–45 (transit passage).** UN Division for Ocean Affairs. [link](https://www.un.org/Depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part3.htm) — **Grade A1**. Summary: The legal rules for passing through straits, including the right of transit passage and Article 43, which says user and coastal states "should by agreement co-operate". The legal basis for all three straits. Reliability: The primary treaty text, though Iran signed but never ratified it.
- **Treves, T. (2009). Piracy, law of the sea, and use of force: developments off the coast of Somalia.** European Journal of International Law 20(2). [link](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejil/chp027) — **Grade A1**. Summary: Explains how UN Security Council resolutions let navies enter Somali waters, and why navies hesitated to detain pirates over human-rights worries. Useful for the "enforcement of laws" factor. Reliability: Written by a former judge of the international law-of-the-sea tribunal.
- **Guilfoyle, D. (2008). Piracy off Somalia: UN Security Council Resolution 1816 and IMO regional counter-piracy efforts.** International and Comparative Law Quarterly 57(3). [link](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-and-comparative-law-quarterly/article/abs/ii-piracy-off-somalia-un-security-council-resolution-1816-and-imo-regional-counterpiracy-efforts/A7C877DE295732AD8D439457F9AA4978) — **Grade A1**. Summary: Covers Resolution 1816, the IMO-led regional cooperation that became the Djibouti Code of Conduct, and the need for Somalia's consent. Good for the formal frameworks factor. Reliability: A leading law journal and a specialist author; paywalled.
- **Kontorovich, E. and Art, S. (2010). An empirical examination of universal jurisdiction for piracy.** American Journal of International Law 104. [link](https://scholarlycommons.law.northwestern.edu/facultyworkingpapers/38) — **Grade A2**. Summary: Using 1998–2009 data, it finds that at most 1.47% of clearly punishable piracies were prosecuted, even though any state may try pirates. Strong evidence that law on paper is not enforcement. Reliability: Peer-reviewed in the top US law journal; pages and DOI unconfirmed.
- **[Author to check] (n.d.). UNCLOS under fire: recalibrating maritime security governance.** International and Comparative Law Quarterly. [link](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-and-comparative-law-quarterly/article/unclos-under-fire-recalibrating-maritime-security-governance/CC79F3F984435EEFFF580C342A6B751D) — **Grade A2**. Summary: Argues the law of the sea leaves gaps; for example, UNCLOS piracy rules do not apply in territorial waters, where most Malacca attacks happen. Explains why coastal states control enforcement. Reliability: A high-prestige law journal, but the author and year still need checking.

### Cross-case reports and data

- **Verschuur, J., Lumma, J. and Hall, J.W. (2025). Systemic impacts of disruptions at maritime chokepoints.** Nature Communications 16. [link](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-65403-w) — **Grade A1**. Summary: Estimates that about US$192 billion of trade a year is at risk at 24 chokepoints, counting delays, rerouting, insurance and freight. It puts all three of your cases on one scale. Reliability: A top free journal and an Oxford/TU Delft team, though the figure is a model estimate made before the 2026 Hormuz closure.
- **EIA (2024, updated 2026). World Oil Transit Chokepoints.** US Energy Information Administration. [link](https://www.eia.gov/international/analysis/special-topics/World_Oil_Transit_Chokepoints) — **Grade A1**. Reliability: Official US government data, free.
- **UNCTAD (2024). Review of Maritime Transport 2024: Navigating Maritime Chokepoints.** United Nations. [link](https://unctad.org/publication/review-maritime-transport-2024) — **Grade A1**. - **Federal Reserve Bank of St Louis (ongoing). FRED: Crude Oil Prices: Brent – Europe (DCOILBRENTEU).** FRED, source US EIA. [link](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DCOILBRENTEU) — **Grade A1**. Summary: The insurance market's list of high-risk sea areas, showing when each became "high risk" and needed extra war-risk premiums.
