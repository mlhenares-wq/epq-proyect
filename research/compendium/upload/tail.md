
## Limitations and what to check before citing

Every source here was confirmed to exist, but almost none could be opened in full, so check each figure on the original page before you cite it.

1. **Search snippets only.** The research environment blocked direct access to web pages, so summaries and figures come from search-result text. Credibility grades are capped at 2 unless several independent sources agree. Items marked [BK] or "to check" rely on background knowledge and need checking first.
2. **Missing outlets.** The NYT, Guardian, BBC, FT, El País, The Economist and some Indian papers were blocked from search, so other papers from the same countries stand in for them.
3. **The scores are judgements.** The 1–5 bands, the equal weights and the process-tracing scores are reasoned choices, not measurements. The verdicts survive most changes, with one exception: counting national patrols as cooperation makes cooperation the main reason at Malacca.
4. **Thin historical data.** There is no daily ship-tracking data before 2019, no 1967 war-risk premium, and no freight index for the Tanker War, so some periods use fewer indicators.
5. **Likelihood and severity overlap.** Insurance premiums rise with both the chance and the cost of an attack.
6. **The K term assumes cooperation helps.** It shows the best case for cooperation. The process-tracing table, not the equation, judges whether cooperation caused a change.
7. **The 2026 crises are still going on.** The Hormuz and Red Sea duration scores may rise.
8. **Dates and counts that conflict between sources:** the Hormuz re-closures (April, late June and July 2026); Malacca attacks in 2004 (37 or 38); the Eternity C deaths (3 or 4); and the end of the 1974 Suez clearance (15 or 20 December).
