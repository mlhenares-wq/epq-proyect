## Sources: Strait of Malacca

126 sources on Malacca and Singapore Strait piracy, ReCAAP, the joint patrols and the 2025–26 surge and fall. Most regional press is graded C: reliable for local detail and national views, but check figures against ReCAAP or IMB data.

### Peer-reviewed and academic

- **[Authors to check] (2023). Multilateral cooperation against maritime piracy in the Straits of Malacca: from the RMSI to ReCAAP.** *Marine Policy*. [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0308597X23001550) — **Grade A2**. Summary: Explains why the US-backed RMSI plan failed (Indonesia and Malaysia saw it as a threat to their sovereignty) and why ReCAAP, built on sharing information, did better. Reliability: Peer-reviewed journal with the abstract confirmed, but authors unknown, paywalled, and it may favour the ReCAAP model.
- **Johnson, D. and Valencia, M. (eds) (2005). Piracy in Southeast Asia: Status, Issues, and Responses.** ISEAS / IIAS (book). [link](https://bookshop.iseas.edu.sg/publication/744) — **Grade A2**. Summary: Academic book written at the height of the piracy peak, covering its causes, why sovereignty and border disputes slowed cooperation, and the role of shipowners and insurers. Reliability: Scholarly and written during the events, so it ends before ReCAAP and Eyes in the Sky took effect; summary from search results only.
- **Ong-Webb, G.G. (ed.) (2006). Piracy, Maritime Terrorism and Securing the Malacca Straits.** ISEAS / IIAS (book). [link](https://bookshop.iseas.edu.sg/publication/745) — **Grade A2**. Summary: 266-page academic book with a chapter on how ASEAN cooperation (MALSINDO, Eyes in the Sky) developed, published the year ReCAAP came into force. Reliability: Academic but from a Singapore-based publisher, so a Singapore viewpoint, and too early to judge long-term results.
- **Febrica, S. (2014). Securing the Sulu-Sulawesi Seas from maritime terrorism: a troublesome cooperation?** *Perspectives on Terrorism*. [link](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/76406/1/Febrica_POT_2014_Securing_the_Sulu_Sulawesi_sea_from_maratime.pdf) — **Grade A2**. Summary: Argues Indonesia rejected the US RMSI not only over sovereignty but because it seemed too military and Jakarta had no say in designing it. Reliability: Peer-reviewed article (repository copy), but the title is about the Sulu-Sulawesi Seas, so check that the Malacca/RMSI passage is really there.
- **[Authors to check] (2026). Complexities of governance and sovereignty in maritime security.** *Marine Policy*. [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0308597X26000369) — **Grade A3**. Summary: Critiques Eyes in the Sky: about 8 air patrols a week were flown against about 70 needed, in daytime while pirates struck at night, so the fall in piracy does not prove the patrols worked. Reliability: Peer-reviewed journal, but the title and authors are unconfirmed, so check the 8/70 figures first.
- **[Authors to check] (2022). Interstate hostility and maritime crime: evidence from South East Asia.** *Marine Policy*. [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/am/pii/S0308597X22001816) — **Grade A3**. Summary: Statistical study linking bad relations between neighbours (no cross-border hot pursuit, distrust of foreign warships) to more armed robbery at sea: weak cooperation means more incidents. Reliability: Free manuscript of a peer-reviewed paper, but an exact-title search failed, so authors and details are unverified.
- **[Author to check] (2014). Multilateralism in the Straits of Malacca and Singapore.** *Marine Policy*. [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0308597X13001978) — **Grade A6**. Summary: Looks at the multilateral arrangements for the straits, probably including the Cooperative Mechanism between coastal and user states. Reliability: Peer-reviewed journal, but only the title was seen and the content is a guess, so read the abstract first.
- **[Authors and year to check]. How did maritime piracy affect trade in Southeast Asia?** *Journal of East Asian Studies* (Cambridge). [link](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-east-asian-studies/article/how-did-maritime-piracy-affect-trade-in-southeast-asia/E45B5C7651110CC17D89B54316955C34) — **Grade A6**. Summary: Measures whether piracy actually cut trade in Southeast Asia, which shows whether Malacca piracy was a serious disruption or mainly a nuisance. Reliability: Respected Cambridge journal, but only the title was seen, so the author, year and findings are unknown.
- **[Author to check] (2018). Pirates of the South China Seas.** *Asian Affairs*, 49(1). [link](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03068374.2018.1416010) — **Grade A6**. Summary: Background history of piracy in the South China Sea and Malacca region, linking the 2000s peak to recent patterns. Reliability: Journal of the Royal Society for Asian Affairs; author unknown, paywalled, and the summary is general (title-level only).
- **Galgano, F.A. (2023). Hostis humani generis: pirates and global maritime commerce.** *Research in Globalization*, 8. [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2590051X23000783) — **Grade B2**. Summary: Using IMB reports, estimates a fall of nearly 75% in pirate activity in the Malacca and Singapore Straits and nearby Indonesian waters, credited to joint patrols and information sharing. Reliability: A Villanova geographer in a mid-tier open-access journal; other studies (Marine Policy 2026, Heliyon 2023) challenge its credit to cooperation.
- **[Authors to check] (2023). Assessment of global shipping risk caused by maritime piracy.** *Heliyon*. [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405844023081963) — **Grade B2**. Summary: Maps piracy risk worldwide; one snippet says that after 2007 Malacca anti-piracy measures worked but pushed piracy elsewhere in Southeast Asia (moved, not removed). Reliability: Mega-journal with lighter review; authors unknown and the displacement point rests on one snippet.
- **Fernández, M. (2017). Piratería marítima en el Estrecho de Malaca, Somalia y Nigeria: causas, consecuencias y respuestas.** Thesis, Universidad Complutense de Madrid. [link](https://eprints.ucm.es/id/eprint/45559/) — **Grade B2**. Summary: Spanish-language thesis comparing piracy off Indonesia/Malacca, Somalia and Nigeria, useful for setting Malacca's regional model against Somalia's naval one. Reliability: University thesis (level not stated), seen only as a repository record.
- **[Author and year to check]. Security in the Straits of Malacca.** *The Asia-Pacific Journal: Japan Focus*. [link](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/asia-pacific-journal/article/security-in-the-straits-of-malacca/43AB4ED844F0214995F93C67B5FEF272) — **Grade B3**. Summary: Credits both coordinated patrols and better rule of law on land in coastal areas for the fall in piracy. Reliability: Cambridge-hosted journal mixing refereed and editor-reviewed pieces; author and year unknown and it may be old.
- **García Sánchez, P. (2017). La piratería en el estrecho de Malaca.** Grupo de Estudios de Historia Militar (GEHM). [link](https://gehm.es/biblio/La_pirateria_en_el_estrecho_de_Malaca_Pablo_Garcia_gehm.pdf) — **Grade C2**. Summary: Short Spanish study of the strait's geography and piracy history; it gives the strait as about 800 km long and 2.8 km wide at its narrowest. Reliability: Written by a military-history study group and not peer-reviewed, so use it for background only.

### Reports, data and official documents

- **ReCAAP ISC (2026). Piracy and Armed Robbery against Ships in Asia: Annual Report 2025 (and press release).** ReCAAP Information Sharing Centre. [link](https://recaap.org/resources/ck/files/reports/annual/ReCAAP%20ISC%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf) — **Grade A1**. Summary: 132 incidents in Asia in 2025 (+23%). The straits had 108, the most since 2007 (+74%), but 107 were in the Singapore Strait, none were in the worst category, and cases fell after Indonesian arrests. Reliability: Original data from the 21-state body, which wants to show its framework works.
- **ReCAAP ISC (2025). Incident Alert 01/2025: sea robbery incidents in the Singapore Strait.** ReCAAP ISC. [link](https://www.recaap.org/resources/ck/files/alerts/2025/ReCAAP%20ISC%20IA%2001-2025%20Sea%20robbery%20Incidents%20in%20the%20Singapore%20Strait%20(3%20Feb%202025).pdf) — **Grade A1**. Summary: Warned on 3 February 2025 that robberies in the eastbound lane had nearly tripled, with 11 incidents since 1 January. Reliability: Primary warning to shipping that shows how fast the information-sharing system works, though it covers only five weeks.
- **ReCAAP member states (2004). Regional Cooperation Agreement on Combating Piracy and Armed Robbery against Ships in Asia.** NUS Centre for International Law treaty database. [link](https://cil.nus.edu.sg/databasecil/2004-regional-cooperation-agreement-on-combating-piracy-and-armed-robbery-against-ships-in-asia-3/) — **Grade A1**. Summary: Asia's first government-to-government anti-piracy treaty (signed November 2004, in force 4 September 2006), which set up the Information Sharing Centre. Reliability: Primary legal text, but its main limit is that Malaysia and Indonesia, the two biggest coastal states, are not parties.
- **ICC International Maritime Bureau (2001). Piracy attacks rise to alarming new levels, ICC report reveals.** ICC press release. [link](https://iccwbo.org/news-publications/news/piracy-attacks-rise-to-alarming-new-levels-icc-report-reveals/) — **Grade A1**. Summary: Announced the 2000 world record: 469 attacks (+57%) and 72 seafarers killed; the Malacca Strait had 75 attacks, up from 2 in 1999. Reliability: Original data from a shipowner-backed watchdog that wants the threat noticed; IMB and ReCAAP count differently, so don't mix them.
- **ICC International Maritime Bureau (2026). Piracy and Armed Robbery Against Ships: Report for 2025.** ICC-IMB. [link](https://iccwbo.org/news-publications/report/global-maritime-piracy-and-armed-robbery-increased-in-2025/) — **Grade A2**. Summary: 137 incidents worldwide in 2025. The Singapore Straits had 80 (58% of the world total), guns were reported 27 times, and cases fell after Indonesian police caught two gangs in July. Reliability: Industry-funded watchdog data, seen via a media release; its count (80) differs from ReCAAP's (108).
- **Ministry of Defence, Singapore (2005). Joint news release: launch of Eyes in the Sky (EiS) initiative.** National Archives of Singapore. [link](https://www.nas.gov.sg/archivesonline/data/pdfdoc/20050913984.htm) — **Grade B1**. Summary: Malaysia, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand launched joint air patrols on 13 September 2005. Each state flies up to two patrols a week, with an officer from each country on board. Reliability: Official four-state release, solid on what governments did but silent on whether it worked.
- **Ministry of Defence, Singapore (2008). Thailand joins Malacca Straits Patrols.** National Archives of Singapore. [link](https://www.nas.gov.sg/archivesonline/data/pdfdoc/20080918989.htm) — **Grade B1**. Summary: On 18 September 2008 Thailand signed the patrols' revised rules and became the fourth full member, after being an observer from 2005. Reliability: Primary government release, reliable for dates but says nothing about effectiveness.
- **Ministry of Defence, Singapore (2016). Malacca Straits Patrol: Member States commemorate 10 years of cooperation.** MINDEF news release. [link](https://www.mindef.gov.sg/news-and-events/latest-releases/2016apr21-news-releases-01782/) — **Grade B2**. Summary: Singapore credits the Malacca Straits Patrol with getting Lloyd's insurers to drop the strait's war-risk listing in August 2006. Reliability: A self-promoting government look back written 10 years later, so use it as the official claim, not proof of cause.
- **Ministry of Defence / Republic of Singapore Navy (2025). Reply to media query on increase in piracy in Singapore waters.** MINDEF. [link](https://www.mindef.gov.sg/news-and-events/latest-releases/13oct25-mq/) — **Grade B2**. Summary: The navy said nothing happened in Singapore's own waters in 2025, and Singapore Strait robberies fell from 14 in July to 2 in August and 1 in September. Reliability: A government defending its record, and counting only its own territorial waters narrows the numbers.
- **RSIS (2005–2015). Commentaries CO05057 "Malacca Straits: a war risk?", Ho (2009) and CO15082 "Expanding maritime patrols in Southeast Asia".** RSIS, Nanyang Technological University. [link](https://rsis.edu.sg/rsis-publication/rsis/718-malacca-straits-a-war-risk/) — **Grade B2**. Summary: Joshua Ho credits the Malacca Straits Patrols with cutting piracy but warns there is "no guarantee" it won't return; CO05057 dates from the 2005 war-risk listing. Reliability: Respected Singapore think tank close to the government, so it may over-credit the patrols.
- **RSIS / IDSS (2004). Operationalising the Regional Maritime Security Initiative.** IDSS Commentaries. [link](https://rsis.edu.sg/rsis-publication/idss/614-operationalising-the-regional/) — **Grade B2**. Summary: Explains that the RMSI grew from US Admiral Fargo's March 2004 plan for special forces on fast boats, and that the region saw outside patrols as breaking transit rules. Reliability: Written at the time by a Singapore think tank; Singapore was most open to US help, so it may play down opposition.
- **Thư Nguyễn Hoàng Anh (2023). Sea robbery in the Singapore Strait.** *The Interpreter* (Lowy Institute). [link](https://www.lowyinstitute.org/the-interpreter/sea-robbery-singapore-strait) — **Grade B2**. Summary: Using an August 2023 ReCAAP report, describes rising petty theft from ships in the Singapore Strait, showing gaps in coastal-state enforcement despite patrols. Reliability: Blog of an Australian think tank by a Vietnamese researcher, written before the 2025 surge.
- **CIMSEC (c. 2014). It's the economy: exploring Indonesia's piracy problem.** Center for International Maritime Security. [link](https://cimsec.org/economy-stupid-exploring-indonesias-piracy-problem/) — **Grade C2**. Summary: Argues piracy off Indonesia is mainly an economic problem force cannot fix: Indonesian incidents rose from 15 in 2009 to 106 in 2013 while joint patrols ran. Reliability: Naval-affairs forum, not peer-reviewed; the author and date were not seen and the figures are unchecked.
- **East Asia Forum (2024). Insurers and shippers combat piracy in Southeast Asia.** East Asia Forum (ANU). [link](https://eastasiaforum.org/2024/05/17/insurers-and-shippers-combat-piracy-in-southeast-asia/) — **Grade C2**. Summary: Armed robbery around the straits rose after 2015 while ransom piracy fell from 2007 to 2022, and insurers and shipowners now increasingly lead the response. Reliability: University-hosted expert commentary rather than data; authors not recorded and only a snippet was seen.
- **Pacific Forum CSIS (2004). PacNet 29: U.S. RMSI contravenes UN Convention on the Law of the Sea.** Pacific Forum. [link](https://pacforum.org/publications/pacnet-29-u-s-rmsi-contravenes-un-convention-on-the-law-of-the-sea/) — **Grade C3**. Summary: A Malaysian-view argument that US patrols under the RMSI would be illegal without the coastal states' consent; it presents the June 2004 Indonesia–Malaysia patrol deal as a response. Reliability: Short opinion piece pushing a national legal case, useful for 2004 attitudes rather than facts.
