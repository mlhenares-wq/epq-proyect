# P14 — Provenance assessment: regional newspapers, Suez / Red Sea / Bab el-Mandeb

Assesses every source in `research/sources/14-news-redsea-region.md` (98 items) using `research/provenance-rubric.md`: Admiralty grade (source reliability A–F + information credibility 1–6), OPVL note, and usefulness 1–5.

## Method and ground rules

- **No page was opened.** The original researcher saw headlines, URLs and search-engine summaries only, and this assessment could not open pages either (WebFetch blocked). Following the rubric note, **credibility is capped at 2** unless several *independent* sources in the file (or well-known wire reporting) give the same fact or figure. Outlets repeating the same Suez Canal Authority (SCA) figure do not count as independent of each other.
- **State media on their own government = D** (rubric section 1, D row). This applies to Ahram Online and Egypt Today (Egypt), Saba (Houthi authorities), La Nation (Djibouti), Shabait (Eritrea), and to Arab News, Jordan Times and Turkish pro-government outlets *when the story is about their own government's actions or claims*. On other topics, state-aligned national papers are graded C, as the rubric lists Arab News.
- **Wire copy** (Reuters / AP / AFP) republished by a regional paper is graded on the wire agency (B), with the republishing outlet noted.
- **Partisan Yemeni outlets** writing about the Houthis (their enemy) are graded C if they report third-party sources with named attribution (South24, Yemen Monitor), and D if they only repost others' reporting or Houthi claims with no original reporting (Yemen Online).
- **RSF World Press Freedom Index 2026** (published 30 April 2026; 180 countries), found by web search on 20 October 2026: South Africa 21, Israel 116, Ethiopia 148, Sudan 161, Turkey 163, Yemen 164, Djibouti 167, Egypt 169, Saudi Arabia 176 (−14 places; exact rank from a secondary report), Eritrea 180. **Not found** in this session: Jordan, Kenya, Somalia (check rsf.org/en/index).
- OPVL lines use **O** = origin, **P** = purpose, **V** = value, **L** = limitations.

## Use with caution

Use these only for what a government or armed group *claims*, or check them against an A/B source before quoting a figure.

- **Y3** (Saba, D4): claim of an official US/coalition withdrawal conflicts with other reporting. Narrative evidence only.
- **J4** (Jordan Times, D4): minister's "minimal effect" claim conflicts with Aqaba port data in J1–J3.
- **Y10** (Yemen Monitor, C4): 25 ships/week conflicts with Kpler daily counts (E7, IL9). Do not use the figure.
- **Y12** (Yemen Online, D3): Houthi claim of 48 tankers turned back, relayed by a partisan repost site.
- **ER1** (Shabait, D3): Eritrean state propaganda; claims of foreign bases are unverified.
- **TR8** (Daily Sabah op-ed, D3): pro-government advocacy for a state project.
- **E3** (Ahram, D3): SCA sales pitch against the Cape route.
- **D1, D4** (La Nation, D3): undated promotional state features.
- **E1, E2, E4, E5, E12, E13, E14** (Egyptian state and state-aligned, D2): the core Suez revenue and transit figures all come from the SCA through state outlets. They are usable as official figures, but cross-check them with IMF PortWatch or UNCTAD and say which period (fiscal or calendar year) each covers.
- **E10** (Daily News Egypt, C3): $10bn cumulative-loss figure used for lobbying.
- **E18** (Mada Masr, C3): anonymous single source.
- **SO8** (Hiiraan, C3): rival ransom vs rescue claims, unresolved.
- **Y1, Y2** (Saba, D1/D2): Houthi announcements. Cite them as statements of intent only.
- **Y11** (Yemen Online, D2): cite the original Reuters report instead. The Houthis denied the fees plan.
- **S1, S2, S7** (Arab News, D2), **TR4** (Hürriyet, D2), **J5** (Jordan Times, D2), **ER2, ER3** (Shabait, D2), **D2, D3** (La Nation, D2): own-government positions. Good evidence of what each state says, but not of what happened.
- **IL1–IL6**: free press, but the Eilat CEO is the main source and was lobbying for state aid. Treat the damage figures as upper estimates.

## Strongest 5

1. **IL10** — Times of Israel (AP), past Suez closures (**B1**, usefulness 5). This is the cleanest 1967–75 comparison, with the closure length and UN cost estimates. Trace the UN study for the final citation.
2. **E7** — Egypt Independent (Reuters / Kpler), dual-chokepoint risk (**B2**, usefulness 5). It gives independent ship-tracking counts for Bab el-Mandeb and connects the Hormuz and Red Sea case studies.
3. **IL1** — Jerusalem Post (Reuters), Eilat −85% (**B1**, usefulness 4). This is the most-cited direct trade-reduction figure for Israel.
4. **ZA2** — Daily Maverick (Reuters), carriers re-divert after the strikes on Iran (**B1**, usefulness 4). It shows how fragile the recovery was and how long the disruption lasted.
5. **IL4** — Times of Israel, Eilat "state of bankruptcy" (**C1**, usefulness 4). It is corroborated by IL2 and IL5 and covers the severity of the damage to Eilat, with the caveat that the port survived on state aid.

*Close runners-up:* S4 (Reuters, China–Houthi deal, B2), IL9 (AP/AFP, Houthi capture of the strait's islands, B2), E16 and E17 (Mada Masr, cooperation and burden-sharing, C1). **E2** and **S3** carry essential figures (the 2024 Suez collapse and the tripling of Yanbu exports) but come from state-owned or state-aligned outlets. Confirm both against an A-grade dataset before using them in the risk equation.
