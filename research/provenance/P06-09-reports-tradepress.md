# P06–09 – Provenance and reliability: reports, books, data (file 06) and trade press (file 09)

Assessed 20 October 2026 using `research/provenance-rubric.md` (Admiralty Code + OPVL + usefulness 1–5).

**Method and caveats**
- The assessor did not open any of these pages (WebFetch was blocked). Grades rest on the descriptions in files 06 and 09, what is known about each producer, and three web searches on think-tank funding (IISS, Elcano, WINEP). In line with the rubric, credibility is **capped at 2** unless independent sources in the files give the same figure (then **1**). Credibility is **6** where the snippet showed no figure.
- **Primary/Secondary:** "Primary" means original data, a legal or official document, or reporting from the time of the events that carries participants' own statements (most 2023–26 news). "Secondary" means later analysis, synthesis or a re-summary of someone else's report.
- **Reposts:** the rubric grades Hellenic Shipping News reposts **D**. The original publisher (Drewry, Malay Mail/ReCAAP) would grade higher, so cite the original instead.
- **Agency copy:** an Insurance Journal, Rigzone, gCaptain or OE Digital item that carries Reuters or Bloomberg copy is graded on the agency (**B**). Their own staff stories are graded as trade press (**C**). TradeWinds is graded **B** alongside Lloyd's List as the other shipping paper of record.

**Funding and interest notes used below**
- **IISS:** leaked documents (Guardian, 2016) showed about £25m from the Bahraini government over five years, roughly a quarter of income, linked to the Manama Dialogue. IISS did not dispute the documents. This matters for Gulf topics.
- **CSIS and Atlantic Council:** funded partly by the US government, defence contractors and Gulf governments (UAE, Qatar, Saudi Arabia). They tend to favour US-led solutions and the US security role in the Gulf.
- **Brookings:** centrist. It took large Qatari government funding in the past (the Doha centre closed in 2021) and also has corporate donors.
- **RUSI:** UK defence-establishment-linked.
- **ECFR:** pro-EU. Funded by foundations (e.g. Open Society) and European governments.
- **WINEP:** founded in 1985 with AIPAC backing. It is seen as close to Israeli and US security viewpoints, and its donors are not transparent.
- **Real Instituto Elcano:** about 80% of its budget comes from corporate patrons, including **Repsol** (an energy company, so relevant to oil coverage). It also has close links to the Spanish government.
- **RAND, NDU and the Naval War College:** US-government-funded or US-government-run.
- **RSIS and ISAS:** part of Singapore universities, close to the Singapore government.
- **ACLED:** funded partly by governments, including the US State Department. Its methodology is transparent.
- **Trade press:** brokers (Marsh, Howden), insurers (Chubb, Lloyd's), P&I clubs (Gard) and broker-research firms gain when risk is talked up and premiums or demand for cover rise. Freight indices (Drewry WCI, Baltic Exchange TD3C, Freightos) and data firms (Kpler, Vortexa, Clarksons, LLI, Linerlytica) sell commercial products and publish *assessments*, not fixtures actually paid.

### OPVL notes – file 06

**1 Childs/IISS.** O: Nick Childs, IISS senior fellow for naval forces, June 2026. Written mid-crisis. P: inform policymakers. IISS has a Gulf presence and took large Bahraini funding in the past, so watch for a Gulf-state angle. V: the strongest argument on the *limits* of military cooperation ("no plausible military option"). L: opinion, not data. Written before outcomes were known. Only the snippet was seen.

**2 Brookings.** O: Brookings 'Blowback' collection, 2026. Author and date unknown. P: explain oil-market effects to a US policy audience. Centrist, with past Qatari funding. V: Brent path ($100 in March, $118 on 29 April) and transit collapse (8% of normal). These figures agree with IEA (#18) and PortWatch (#39). L: the figures came from a search summary that blended several pages, so check them on the page.

**3 Atlantic Council.** O: issue brief, 2026. US Atlanticist think tank. P: argue that the closure erodes the rules-based order. Funded by the US government, defence industry and Gulf governments, and it favours US/NATO-led answers. V: framing for the "formal frameworks" factor, plus dates (US blockade 13 April; Iranian fee lane 5 May). L: advocacy tone. Funding conflicts on Gulf topics. Dates need checking against agency news.

**4 CSIS 2026 pieces.** O: CSIS, 2026. Authors not shown. P: analysis for Washington. Funded partly by the US government, defence contractors and Gulf states. V: even China could not get through, so diplomatic ties did not protect trade. Also LNG effects, ready-made charts, and the claim that the MoU "collapsed" in July. L: there may be a US-centric framing of Iran and China. Authors unknown. Only snippets were seen.

**5 Elcano 2026.** O: Spain's Real Instituto Elcano, early 2026. Author and date not seen. P: inform the Spanish and EU policy audience. Corporate patrons include Repsol, which has an interest in energy-security coverage. V: Spain/EU angle on supply-chain and energy dependence. L: short comment, written early (before the outcome). Metadata missing. In Spanish.

**6 RUSI/WOTR webinar.** O: recorded panel, 31 July 2026 (Burns, Hopton, Ziemba). P: expert discussion for members and the public. RUSI is linked to the UK defence establishment. V: links the Hormuz and Bab al-Mandab crises (connected crises). L: spoken opinion, possibly behind a members' wall. Not seen. Hard to cite precisely.

**7 Revista General de Marina.** O: Spanish Navy journal, June 2026. Author not given. P: professional education for naval officers. Official Spanish defence viewpoint. V: Spanish-language view on trade concentration. L: not peer-reviewed and largely descriptive. Spanish institutional framing. Only a snippet was seen.

**8 CSIS 2024 Red Sea.** O: CSIS, early 2024. Written while events were happening. P: assess economic impact for US policymakers (funding as in #4). V: early counts (~40 attacks), Cape detour cost (up to $1m per trip), insurer withdrawals, and the January 2024 strikes. L: early snapshot whose numbers were later overtaken. The "$1m" is an estimate.

**9 CSIS 2026 Houthis.** O: CSIS, 2026. P: warn about escalation. Its focus on US and Saudi interests matches its donor base. V: key to "alternative factors": the East–West pipeline to Yanbu is near capacity and exposed to the Houthis. This is backed up by trade press R19. L: argues from scenarios. Gulf-donor sensitivity on Saudi issues.

**10 ECFR.** O: European Council on Foreign Relations, 2026, plus related briefs (September 2026). P: push for more EU resources for Aspides and Atalanta. Pro-EU, funded by foundations and governments. V: calls EU missions "fragmented and under-resourced", which is direct evidence for evaluating Aspides. L: advocacy, so it has a reason to stress under-resourcing in order to argue for more.

**11 Elcano 2024.** O: Elcano *Análisis*, 2024. P: analysis for Spanish policy. Energy-company patrons. V: about 70% of Red Sea tankers diverted, but the oil-market effect was limited, which makes a useful contrast with Hormuz. Also Spain's CTF-153 link. L: early-2024 estimate. The tanker share is unverified. In Spanish.

**12 WINEP.** O: Washington Institute, 2025. P: policy analysis. Founded with AIPAC backing, close to Israeli and US security viewpoints, and donors are not disclosed. V: timeline of attack patterns (pause on 19 January 2025, then resumption). L: partisan lens on Iran and the Houthis. Graded C. Check counts against ACLED (#13).

**13 ACLED.** O: ACLED year-end report, December 2025. P: provide conflict-event data. Partly government-funded, with a transparent coding method. V: Houthi attacks fell 84% from 2024 to 2025, a likelihood measure over time. L: coding rules shape the counts. Free registration needed. Not cross-checked.

**14 Chalk/RAND.** O: Peter Chalk, RAND, 2008, soon after the 2004 MALSINDO patrols. P: advise the US government (RAND is a US-government-funded FFRDC). V: causes of the rise in piracy and a review of the response initiatives for the Malacca case. L: US policy lens. Written before ReCAAP had matured. Only a library record was seen.

**15 RSIS.** O: Singapore's RSIS (NTU), 2005–2015. P: commentary for the regional policy community. Close to the Singapore government. V: CO05057 dates from the 2005 JWC listing, so it is evidence from the time on the insurance measure. Ho credits the patrols. L: Singapore-government lens, so it may credit the patrols too much. Short pieces. No 2025 piece.

**16 NDU Press.** O: National Defense University Press, 2022. Author not shown. P: draw lessons for US forces. V: Earnest Will lessons (no advance minesweepers; *Bridgeton* mined). Navias (#30) and Zatarain (#32) back this up. L: US military viewpoint. Author and title must be taken from the page before citing.

**17 EIA.** O: US Energy Information Administration, updated June 2024, using Vortexa data. P: official energy statistics. V: the standard volumes per chokepoint. Malacca (23.7 mb/d) is above Hormuz, which matches M8. L: 2023 data from before the 2026 war. The 2026 update has not been confirmed. Relies on a commercial tanker-tracking firm.

**18 IEA OMR.** O: IEA, Paris, April–August 2026. P: the IEA's mandate is energy security for oil-consuming OECD members (set up in 1974; it coordinates emergency stock releases), so it has an interest in justifying the stock release it led. V: monthly hard data (loadings 3.8 vs >20 mb/d; 14.4 mb/d shut in; 1 bn+ barrels lost). L: full report paywalled. The May forecast of resumption was wrong. Only snippets were seen.

**19 World Bank CMO.** O: World Bank, April 2026. Seen only via Kitco coverage. P: forecasts for member governments. Its mandate centres on development and commodity-importing poorer countries. V: "largest oil supply shock on record", Hormuz carries ~35% of seaborne crude, Brent $86 base / $115 downside, energy prices +24%. L: the baseline (normal by October 2026) was proved wrong. Seen at second hand only, so cite the PDF.

**20 OECD Interim.** O: OECD, March 2026. Seen via a mirror and a Swedish delegation summary. P: macroeconomic forecasts for members. V: best inflation measure (G20 4.0%, +1.2 pts). L: an early forecast, possibly revised in June. The original page was not checked.

**21 UNCTAD RMT 2024.** O: UN Trade and Development, 2024. Its mandate is the trade interests of developing countries, so it stresses the costs to SIDS and LDCs. P: annual reference report on shipping. V: Gulf of Aden tonnage −76%, Suez −70%, Cape +89%. These match IMF (#24) and Clarksons. L: mid-2024 data, so it does not cover 2025–26.

**22 UNCTAD RMT 2025.** O: UNCTAD, September 2025. P: as #21. V: distances +5.9% against volumes +2.2%, which shows the cost of the Cape detour and the length of the disruption. L: figures came through trade coverage (IIMS/Breakbulk), not the PDF itself.

**23 ECB.** O: ECB staff, March 2024. P: its price-stability mandate means it has a reason to present supply shocks as "contained". V: Red Sea inflation effect only +0.25 pts, against Hormuz's much larger effect. L: a model scenario, euro area only, and early.

**24 IMF blog.** O: IMF staff, March 2024, using PortWatch. P: a public chart to explain trade disruption. The IMF's mandate is global financial stability. V: Suez −50% y/y and Cape +74%, confirmed by UNCTAD. L: a blog, not a full paper. Authors must be added. Covers only January–February 2024.

**25 UNSC 2722.** O: UN Security Council, 10 January 2024. P: an official condemnation, the product of great-power bargaining. V: the formal framework for the Red Sea case. Four abstentions (Russia, China, Algeria, Mozambique) show weak consensus. L: wording shaped by negotiation. No enforcement power. The May 2026 Hormuz draft is not verified.

**26 EU Aspides decisions.** O: Council of the EU, 2024–26. P: legal mandate for a defensive mission. V: shows exactly what Aspides may do, plus its duration, budget (€15m against €17m) and added tasks. L: legal text says nothing about effectiveness. The budget figure needs checking in the text.

**27 Atalanta decision.** O: Council of the EU, December 2024. P: extend and widen the mandate. V: Atalanta–Aspides "synergies", the MSC rename, and the Spanish angle (Rota). L: the formal text only. Its relevance to the Red Sea is indirect.

**28 ReCAAP 2025.** O: ReCAAP ISC, a 21-state intergovernmental body, January 2026. P: information sharing. As the framework being evaluated, it has a reason to present itself as effective and to downplay the threat. V: 108 SOMS incidents, but 107 were in the Singapore Strait, with no CAT 1 cases and a fall after Indonesian arrests. Confirmed by M4–M6. L: covers Asia only. Its incident definitions differ from IMB's (#29).

**29 ICC-IMB 2025.** O: ICC International Maritime Bureau, 2026. Seen via a P&I club's copy of the media release. P: the business federation's piracy reporting centre, funded by industry, which has some interest in keeping piracy visible. V: global comparison, plus guns (27) and Somalia contained. L: counts differ from ReCAAP (80 vs 108). Full report on request.

**30 Navias and Hooton.** O: academic historians, 1996, I.B. Tauris. Reviewed in *Iranian Studies*. P: scholarly history. V: index of the ships attacked, so incident numbers for the 1980–88 case. L: dated (1996). May duplicate plan link 8. Library access needed.

**31 Walker/NWC.** O: George K. Walker, US Naval War College *International Law Studies*, 2000. P: legal analysis. V: law of neutral shipping, reflagging and UNSC 598 for "formal frameworks" in the 1980s. Free full text. L: US Navy institution, so a US legal viewpoint.

**32 Zatarain.** O: Lee Allen Zatarain, Casemate, 2008/2010. P: popular narrative for general readers. V: a detailed Earnest Will timeline drawing on Pentagon documents and interviews. L: not academic. US-centric. The edition needs checking.

**33 Johnson and Valencia.** O: ISEAS/IIAS edited volume, 2005, written at the piracy peak. P: academic analysis. V: obstacles to regional cooperation (sovereignty) and the roles of insurers and shipowners. L: ends before ReCAAP and Eyes in the Sky took effect. Access via chapters.

**34 Ong-Webb.** O: ISEAS/IIAS, 2006. P: academic. Singapore-based publisher. V: the progress of intra-ASEAN cooperation (MALSINDO, Eyes in the Sky). L: too early to judge long-term effectiveness. Singapore viewpoint.

**35 Bueger and Edmunds.** O: leading maritime-security scholars, OUP, 2024. P: academic introduction. V: the "type of threat" framework (blue crime vs state-backed actors) explains why the cases differ. L: theory, not case data. Written before 2026.

**36 Till.** O: Geoffrey Till (KCL), Routledge, 5th edn, February 2026. P: textbook. V: theory on good order at sea and naval cooperation. L: general. Published before the Hormuz closure. Page counts vary between listings.

**37 Fishman.** O: Edward Fishman, former US sanctions official, Penguin, 2025. P: trade non-fiction arguing for a US economic-statecraft view. V: context on why Iran uses its leverage, and the shadow fleet. L: about economic chokepoints, not straits. Insider bias. Cited via Wikipedia, so find the publisher page.

**38 ISAS.** O: Institute of South Asian Studies (NUS), March 2025. P: policy research with a South Asian (India-leaning) regional lens. V: alternative ports (Duqm, Gwadar, Chabahar) as physical alternatives. L: only the catalogue entry was seen. Access unknown. Written before 2026.

**39 PortWatch.** O: IMF with the University of Oxford, an AIS-based daily dataset. P: open data for monitoring trade. V: lets the student make their own transit graphs for every case since 2019. L: AIS under-counts dark ships (see H12). Baselines vary (75–130/day). Data arrives late. State the filter used.

**40 FRED Brent.** O: St Louis Fed republishing EIA data. P: public statistics. V: daily Brent since 1987, so comparable across the late Tanker War and 2023–26. L: no daily data before 1987. Spot prices react to more than the chokepoint.

**41 Drewry WCI.** O: Drewry, a commercial consultancy. Weekly index since 2011. P: marketing for a paid product. Drewry gains from attention to volatility. V: the freight-rate measure for the Red Sea case. L: an index *assessment*, not contract rates. The URL is unconfirmed. Seen mostly via Hellenic reposts (R3).

**42 JWC JWLA-033.** O: Lloyd's Market Association Joint War Committee, 3 March 2026. P: underwriter guidance. The market sets risk zones that let it charge additional premiums, so it has a commercial interest in wide listings. V: dates exactly when areas became "high risk". Gard (H3) confirms it. L: says nothing about price levels. The Malacca 2005–06 listing dates are unverified. **Same document as H1.**

## Use with caution

1. **Atlantic Council (06 #3) and CSIS (06 #4, #8, #9):** funded by the US government, defence industry and Gulf governments, so treat their framing of Iran, the Houthis and Saudi Arabia carefully. Use them for facts that other sources confirm.
