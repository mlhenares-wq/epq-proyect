# P08–10 — Provenance and reliability: Spanish sources (file 08) and long-form analysis (file 10)

Assessed 20 October 2026 using `research/provenance-rubric.md` (Admiralty Code: source reliability A–F plus information credibility 1–6; OPVL; usefulness 1–5).

## How these grades were set

- **Nothing in either file was opened in full.** Both compilers worked from search-result snippets only. Following the rubric's project note, credibility is **capped at 2** unless several independent sources give the same fact (then 1). Where only a title or menu was seen, credibility is **6**.
- **No new web searches were run for this assessment.** Author credentials marked [BK] in file 10 remain unconfirmed; they are treated as plausible but should be checked on bio pages.
- **Reliability is graded on the producer, not the conclusion.** For entries that bundle several outlets, the grade is set by the strongest producer actually carrying the key fact (for example EFE, Reuters, or a ministry), and the weaker co-listed outlets are named in the OPVL note.
- **Opinion and analysis are arguments, not evidence.** For opinion, analysis and essays, the credibility digit rates the factual claims the piece rests on, not whether its argument is right. Cite them as "X argues that…", and take figures from the data sources they cite.
- **Spanish editorial leanings used here:** *El País* centre-left; *ABC* and *El Mundo* centre-right; *La Razón* right (none of these three appear in file 08, which is itself a gap); *El Español* centre-right; *The Objective* right-leaning; *Vozpópuli* liberal-right; *Esdiario* right-leaning; *Ara* Catalan, pro-independence and centre-left; *Última Hora* Balearic regional; EFE is the state-owned news agency; Europa Press is a private wire. **IEEE** is part of the Spanish Ministry of Defence (via CESEDEN), so on Spanish defence policy it speaks from inside government. **Real Instituto Elcano** is part-funded by the government and large Spanish companies. **CIDOB** is funded partly by Barcelona and Catalan public bodies. The *Revista General de Marina* is the Spanish Navy's own journal.
- **Type key:** TT = think-tank; Gov = government; PR = peer-reviewed; News; Opinion; Analysis; Trade = trade press or association. **P** = primary (made at the time by participants or as original data/contemporary report); **S** = secondary (analysis after the fact).
