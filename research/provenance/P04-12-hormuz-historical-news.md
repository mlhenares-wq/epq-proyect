# P04-12 – Provenance assessment: Hormuz news (file 04) and historical news (file 12)

Assessed 20 October 2026 using `research/provenance-rubric.md` (Admiralty Code A–F / 1–6, OPVL, usefulness 1–5).

## How the grades were set

- **Producer (letter):** graded as the rubric says. UPI, Reuters, AP, Bloomberg and the Washington Post are graded **B** (major wire agencies or papers of record). *Time* is graded **B**: the rubric does not list it, but it was the leading US news weekly, with its own correspondents and fact-checking. CNBC is **B** (a major financial news broadcaster) and France 24 is **B** (a public international broadcaster, but see the note on France's stake). The *Christian Science Monitor*, Al Jazeera, *The National*, *The Spectator*, *Aramco World*, Merca2 and Moncloa.com are graded **C**. UN News is graded **A** where it reports the IMO's own figures, because the IMO produces those figures itself (like UNCTAD in the rubric). Xinhua and a radio-station repost are **D**. An outlet that could not be identified is **F**.
- **Reposts:** a faithful reprint of wire copy *in a real printed newspaper* (the Michigan Daily archive) is graded as the wire agency, because the scan is the original printed text. A web repost of unattributed wire copy (wwbl.com) is graded **D**, as the rubric says, until the original can be found.
- **Content (number):** no page in either file was opened in full, so the rubric caps credibility at **2**. A source gets **1** only when several independent sources (or other files in this project) report the same event or figure. It gets **3** when the date or attribution is unclear, or when the figure stands alone. It gets **4** when the figure conflicts with better sources.
- **Wartime claims:** 1980s reports that rest on Pentagon, Iraqi or Iranian statements, and 2026 reports that rest on IRGC, CENTCOM or other belligerent statements, are noted as *claims*. They count as evidence of the event only when an independent source confirms them.
- **Primary/Secondary:** a news report written at the time is **primary** for its event. Chronologies, explainers and features that compile earlier reporting are marked **secondary**, even when they were written while the events were still going on.
- **Duplicates:** several UPI stories appear in both files. They get the same Admiralty grade in both. Usefulness can differ, because each file uses them for something different.

### What the 8 verification searches found (20 Oct 2026)
1. **Merca2's "about 80%" figure (04 #37):** Reuters, citing Kpler on 8 October, put the *crude volume* crossing the strait at about **74%** of pre-war levels, while only **7 cargo ships** crossed that day. Merca2 is probably measuring oil volume rather than ship transits. It is not simply wrong, but it is not comparable with PortWatch's ship counts ([gbm.com/Reuters, 8 Oct](https://gbm.com/media/noticia/los-transitos-por-ormuz-caen-a-su-minimo-en-mas-de-dos-meses-tras-ataques-segun-datos/)).
2. **"6,000 seafarers trapped" (04 #39):** this figure was already in circulation in **July–August 2026** (Malay Mail 24 Jul, Al-Monitor, QNA 29 Aug, Qatar Tribune), from the IMO and the UN. The figure is therefore well confirmed, but it is not new as of October. The "24 ships attacked in two weeks" figure was not confirmed ([Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/world/2026/07/24/un-pleads-for-stranded-sailors-rescue-as-hormuz-war-leaves-6000-trapped-at-sea/228843); [QNA](https://qna.org.qa/en/News-Area/News/2026-8/29/un-maritime-agency-says-6000-seafarers-stranded-in-the-strait-of-hormuz)).
3. **IRGC "closed again" around 17 October (04 #40):** no independent report of an October closure was found. The searches did confirm **two IRGC closures that file 04 leaves out of its timeline**: 18–19 April, after a reopening was announced (Al Jazeera, The Week), and **12 July**, "until further notice", reported by Reuters from IRIB ([The Star/Reuters](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/07/12/iran039s-irgc-navy-says-strait-of-hormuz-closed-until-further-notice-state-media-reports)).
4. **Tankers hitting mines:** the reports from 18 July, 2 September, 15 September and 12 October all rest on **IRGC statements**. CENTCOM disputed at least two of them: it said El Gaia was hit by a missile and a drone, not a mine, and called one IRGC claim "false". The NewsNation item cited in 04 #40 therefore needs this caveat ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2651361)).
5. **Capital News Point:** no information about who owns or runs the outlet could be found.
6. **The Lloyd's counts of 447 vessels / 178 in 1987 (04 #14):** no source was found for them. Other Lloyd's-based totals differ: MEES summarising Lloyd's List gives about 550 vessels for the whole war; CSM 1986 gives 259 attacks up to September 1986 ([MEES](https://archives.mees.com/issues/1275/articles/44434)).
7. **The Suez clearance totals (12 #5):** neither Time figure (700,000+ or 686,000) was found. US Navy records give clearance as complete on 20 December 1974 ([USNI Proceedings 1976](https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/1976/february/nimrod-spar-clearing-suez-canal)).
