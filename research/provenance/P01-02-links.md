# Provenance assessment: links 1–34 (Hormuz; Suez / Red Sea / Malacca)

Assessed 20 October 2026 against `research/provenance-rubric.md` (Admiralty Code + OPVL + usefulness 1–5).

**Basis of assessment.** This assessment works from the two link-check files (`sources/01-links-hormuz.md`, `sources/02-links-suez-malacca.md`). No page was opened directly, either in those checks or here. Everything rests on search-result indexing and snippets. Following the rubric note, information credibility is **capped at 2** unless several independent sources give the same figure, or the source is itself the original data. Pages whose content was never seen get **6**. Two publisher look-ups were made by web search, for Iraqi News and Energy Intelligence.

**Conventions used here.**
- *Primary* means the source was produced by a participant or by the body that owns the data: a government, an international organisation, the official in charge, or the data itself.
- *Secondary* means analysis or reporting of someone else's data or actions. This includes news stories that relay tracker data.
- A wire story republished elsewhere is graded on its original producer (Reuters = B). The OPVL note gives the republisher.

### OPVL notes (Section A)

**1. BIS WP 1378 (B2, 5)**
- **O:** E. Kharroubi, Bank for International Settlements, Basel, about 17 Sep 2026. Secondary econometric study built on ship-tracking data.
- **P:** Central-bank research to inform policymakers. The views are the author's own (standard disclaimer).
- **V:** It quantifies Hormuz's stagflationary effect (energy and fertiliser prices, industrial production, CPI, credit spreads, 4–6 month lag), compares chokepoints, and gives hard severity measures.
- **L:** Not peer-reviewed. Seen only via snippets and *Central Banking* coverage. Very recent, so not yet tested by other studies.

**2. Hellenic Shipping News ?p=1131337 (D6, 1)**
- **O:** Piraeus-based English-language shipping site (founded 2006). Post date and author are unknown.
- **P:** Industry news aggregation, mostly reposting releases and other outlets' reports.
- **V:** None identifiable yet. At best it points to an original source (Reuters, broker, IEA, IMO).
- **L:** Post ID not found, so the content is unknown. It is an aggregator with no original reporting. Cite the original instead.

**3. Japan MOD joint statement (B2, 4)**
- **O:** Ministry of Defense of Japan, posted 14 May 2026. It hosts a multinational joint statement (about 25 signatories) from the UK–France-led Hormuz mission process.
- **P:** To record and publicise the states' political commitment to freedom of navigation.
- **V:** Primary evidence of a formal cooperation framework and who joined it (Hormuz 2026, cooperation factor).
- **L:** Shows intent, not outcomes. Japan stated it was not committing to military participation. Signatory list seen only in snippets. Do not describe it as a bilateral Japan–UK meeting.

**4. IEA stock release (A1, 5)**
- **O:** International Energy Agency, Paris, 11 Mar 2026. Primary press release.
- **P:** To announce and justify coordinated action to markets and member governments.
- **V:** Hard figures: 400 mb release; Hormuz exports below 10% of pre-conflict levels; about 20 mb/d (about 25% of seaborne oil) in 2025. A clear case of multilateral cooperation reducing severity.
- **L:** Institutional framing, written to calm markets. Gives the decision, not its effect. Pair it with price data.

**5. Gulf News "jump 44%" (C2, 3)**
- **O:** UAE regional daily, about 13 Aug 2026. It relays commercial tracker data (MarineTraffic or Kpler; accounts differ).
- **P:** Daily news for a Gulf readership. The headline is written to attract readers.
- **V:** A daily traffic snapshot (9 to 13 crossings against a pre-war 100–140), plus evidence that ships used Iran's routing scheme (type of threat).
- **L:** The headline overstates recovery (traffic was still about 90% below normal). The data source is unclear. Exact date unconfirmed. A UAE outlet may reflect Gulf-state perspectives.

**6. Talmadge, "Closing Time" (A2, 5)**
- **O:** Caitlin Talmadge, *International Security* (MIT Press / Belfer Center), 2008. Secondary scholarly analysis.
- **P:** Academic assessment of Iran's military ability to close Hormuz, for a scholarly and policy audience.
- **V:** The key pre-2026 benchmark: full closure was unlikely to last, but mines, missiles and small boats could cause costly, prolonged disruption. Testable against 2026, and it links the Tanker War with the present.
- **L:** Written in 2008, so it predates current Iranian capabilities and the events of 2026. US-centric framing. Read via the free Belfer PDF, not snippets.

**7. O'Neil & Talmadge correspondence (A3, 3)**
- **O:** W.D. O'Neil, with a reply from Talmadge, *International Security* 33(3), 2008/09.
- **P:** Scholarly debate. It challenges Talmadge's weighting of the costs Iran itself would bear.
- **V:** Adds the attacker-cost and deterrence dimension to likelihood. Pairs with link 6 for evaluation.
- **L:** The detailed arguments were not visible in snippets, hence credibility 3. Cite it as O'Neil's reply, not as a Talmadge study. Narrow scope.

**8. BVD record 485712 (F6, 1)**
- **O:** Spanish Ministry of Defence virtual library catalogue. The underlying document is unknown.
- **P:** Catalogue access to a holding.
- **V:** None until the actual document is identified.
- **L:** Record not found. A catalogue entry is not itself citable. Open it, then cite the real author, title and year.

**9. Chatham House expert comment (B2, 4)**

- **L:** Opinion, not research. The specific lessons were cut off in the snippet. Written before the coalition launched, if it ever did. UK perspective.

### OPVL notes (Section B)

**22. Wikipedia, Suez clearance (D2, 2)**
- **O:** Anonymous, crowd-edited online encyclopedia. Undated, with a live revision history. Tertiary.
- **P:** General reference.
- **V:** A quick outline of Nimbus Star, Nimbus Moon and Nimrod Spar (the canal reopened 5 Jun 1975), and a route to the primary sources (links 23 and 27).
- **L:** No accountable author or original reporting, so it is graded D as an aggregator. Its facts are mostly corroborated by US Navy sources, but the end date of clearance differs (15–20 Dec 1974). Do not cite it in the essay.

**23. Boyd, "Nimrod Spar" (B2, 4)**
- **O:** Capt. J. Huntly Boyd Jr., US Navy Supervisor of Salvage, who ran the wreck removal. USNI *Proceedings*, Feb 1976. Primary.
- **P:** Professional account for a naval readership, explaining and showcasing the operation.
- **V:** First-hand detail on the ten wrecks, the contractor (Murphy Pacific) and US–Egypt cost arrangements. Good on joint operations and on the length of the disruption.
- **L:** Likely to be self-congratulatory and US-centric. The OCR text may contain errors. The issue date is disputed (Jan vs Feb 1976; use Feb). Not peer-reviewed.

**24. Feyrer, Suez natural experiment (A2, 5)**
- **O:** James Feyrer (Dartmouth). NBER WP 2009, then SED 2011, then *Journal of Development Economics* 153 (2021), DOI 10.1016/j.jdeveco.2021.102708. Secondary.
- **P:** Academic economics: identifying the effect of distance on trade and of trade on income.
- **V:** The key quantitative evidence on the trade and income cost of a long chokepoint closure (Suez 1967–75). Peer-reviewed in its final form.
- **L:** The plan links the SED version, so cite the 2021 article. Findings were seen via abstracts only. The 1967–75 world economy differs from today's, so generalise with care.

**25. Feyrer, VoxEU column (B2, 4)**
- **O:** Feyrer, VoxEU/CEPR, Dec 2009. A secondary research summary by the author himself.
- **P:** To communicate research to policymakers and the public.
- **V:** Plain-English, quotable figures: a 10% fall in sea distance goes with about a 5% rise in trade, and each extra dollar of trade adds about $0.25 of income.
- **L:** Summarises a pre-publication version. Not refereed. Not independent of link 24, so it does not corroborate it. Use the clean URL.

**26. Notteboom et al., Red Sea Crisis (A2, 4)**
- **O:** Notteboom, Haralambides and Cullinane, *Maritime Economics & Logistics* 26(1):1–20, Mar 2024, DOI 10.1057/s41278-024-00287-z. Secondary.
- **P:** An expert editorial for academics and industry on how the Red Sea crisis affected operations, networks and supply chains.
- **V:** Leading port economists on Cape diversions, liner network redesign and supply-chain effects (Red Sea 2023–26: physical alternatives, supply-chain factor).
- **L:** An editorial, so probably not fully refereed. Full text was not seen. Possibly paywalled. Written early in the crisis.

**27. CTF 65 Final Report (B1, 5)**
- **O:** Commander Task Force 65, US Naval Inshore Warfare Command Atlantic, May 1975. Official, primary, archived by DTIC.
- **P:** An official after-action record and lessons learned for the US Navy.
- **V:** The authoritative record of the 1974 clearance: dates (11 Apr–15 Dec 1974), methods (helicopter mine countermeasures), logistics. Strong on response speed, joint operations and technology.
- **L:** US viewpoint, with UK, French and Egyptian roles under-reported. Self-assessment. Only the front matter and contents were seen. GlobalSecurity gives an end date of 20 Dec.

**28. UNCTAD, *Navigating Troubled Waters* (A1, 5)**
- **O:** UNCTAD, Geneva, 22 Feb 2024. Rapid assessment with original data. Primary.
- **P:** To inform governments about trade disruption in the Red Sea, Black Sea and Panama Canal.
- **V:** Headline severity data: Suez transits down 42% from peak and weekly container transits down 67%. It also separates the Panama drought from the effects of attacks.
- **L:** An early-2024 snapshot, not an annual figure. The 82% container-tonnage figure appears only in secondary coverage, with differing baselines, so verify it in the PDF. Short and not peer-reviewed.

**29. Container News, ASPIDES warning (C2, 3)**
- **O:** Container News trade press, about late March 2026. Relays an EUNAVFOR ASPIDES advisory.
- **P:** Industry news for shipping professionals.
- **V:** Threat levels (medium, and high for Israel-linked ships) and the admission that ASPIDES lacked escort assets. Shows the limits of joint operations.
- **L:** Second-hand, with the exact date unconfirmed. Superseded by later advisories (Jul 2026). Cite the ASPIDES/MSCIO advisory directly.

**30. Energy Intelligence article (C6, 1)**
- **O:** Energy Intelligence Group, a privately held, subscription-only energy publisher (formed in 1996 from PIW and Oil Daily). Article ID suggests about 7 Feb 2024. Title and author unknown.
- **P:** Paid market intelligence for oil and gas executives.
- **V:** Unknown. Probably early-2024 Red Sea tanker coverage.
- **L:** Not verified and paywalled. The producer is reputable within trade press, but the content cannot be judged. Replace it with IMF PortWatch data (free, grade A).

**31. Reuters via Chronicle.lu (B2, 3)**
- **O:** Reuters, 19 Aug 2026, republished by Chronicle.lu (that page was not indexed; the story was found on Arab News, Al-Monitor and others). Secondary report of Kpler data.
- **P:** Wire news for markets.
- **V:** Hormuz traffic of 6 ships against an 11-ship average. Bab el-Mandeb at 30 transits with no Saudi oil, plus the 20 Jul Houthi blockade of Saudi Arabia. Good for the "connected crises" factor.
- **L:** Mainly a Hormuz story misfiled under Red Sea. Daily figures are volatile and now dated. Credit Reuters.

**32. Raymond, *NWCR* 2009 (B3, 4)**
- **O:** Catherine Zara Raymond, *Naval War College Review* 62(3), Summer 2009, pp. 30–42. Open access on JSTOR. Secondary.
- **P:** Professional analysis, for a military and policy readership, of whether Malacca piracy was "solved".
- **V:** Directly evaluates whether cooperation (MALSINDO, Eyes in the Sky, ReCAAP) caused the post-2005 fall. This is the core Malacca evidence.
- **L:** Text not read, so credibility 3 until the student reads it (it is free). Edited professional journal, not fully academic peer review. Ends in 2009.

**33. Beckman, NUS CIL slides (B2, 4)**
- **O:** Prof. Robert Beckman, Centre for International Law, NUS, 27 Feb 2012. Presentation to China's State Oceanic Administration (North China Sea Branch).
- **P:** An expert briefing on piracy trends and legal and cooperative responses.
- **V:** Incident numbers (37 Malacca attacks in 2004) and the timeline of responses (MALSINDO 2004, Eyes in the Sky 2005, Lloyd's delisting 2006).
- **L:** Slides lack context. The 2004 figure conflicts slightly with IMB's 38. The 2008 figure (2 attacks) was not seen in the deck. Back it up with IMB or ReCAAP reports.

**34. Malay Mail, 2025 piracy surge (C1, 3)**
- **O:** *Malay Mail* (Malaysia), 26 Aug 2025, drawing on a *Guardian* report of ReCAAP data. Secondary.
- **P:** National news.
- **V:** Present-day incident numbers (80 in H1 2025 against 21 in H1 2024), confirmed independently by ReCAAP and Singapore's MPA figures. Mostly petty theft.
- **L:** Second-hand, and the headline exaggerates the threat to trade. Overtaken by later data (108 for all of 2025; 28 in H2 after arrests). Cite ReCAAP ISC directly.
