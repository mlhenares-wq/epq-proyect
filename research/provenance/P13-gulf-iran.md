# P13 – Provenance assessment: news from Iran, the Gulf states, Iraq and Pakistan

Assesses every source in `research/sources/13-news-gulf-iran.md` (77 items) using `research/provenance-rubric.md`: Admiralty Code (A–F reliability + 1–6 credibility), OPVL, and usefulness 1–5.
Assessed 20 October 2026.

## How the grades were set

- **Snippet cap.** No article was opened (all pages were blocked in the research session), so credibility is capped at **2**. A **1** is given only where several independent sources give the same fact or figure, as the rubric allows.
- **State media rule.** State agencies, state broadcasters and state-funded outlets (Press TV, IRNA, Iran Daily, Tasnim, Mehr, Kayhan, Tehran Times, WAM, SPA, KUNA/BNA texts, Al Jazeera) get **D** when the item is about **their own government's** actions, claims or diplomacy. As the rubric says, use them only as evidence of what that government *claims*. A neutral factual item, such as third-party data or a story with no stake for the owner, can be graded higher (C or B).
- **Government-aligned private press** (The National, Gulf News, Khaleej Times, Arab News, Asharq Al-Awsat, Saudi Gazette, The Peninsula, Gulf Times, Omani and Bahraini dailies, Kuwait Times) gets **C**, following the rubric's examples. Where such a paper simply reprints a state-agency text or official briefing about its own government, the item is treated as state media (**D**).
- **Iran International** is London-based, opposition-leaning and reportedly Saudi-funded. It counts as a partisan outlet on the Islamic Republic: **D** for framing items, **C** when it relays checkable third-party data or statements.
- **RSF World Press Freedom Index** (rank out of 180). 2026 ranks were confirmed by search for Iran (177), Saudi Arabia (176), Bahrain (170), Iraq (162), Qatar (75) and Oman (127). For the UAE (164), Kuwait (128), Pakistan (158) and the UK (20), the 2025 ranks are used because the 2026 figures were not found. Apart from Qatar, every Gulf state, Iran, Iraq and Pakistan sits in RSF's "difficult" or "very serious" bands.
