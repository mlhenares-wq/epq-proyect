# 13 – News from the countries around the Strait of Hormuz (Iran, Gulf states, Iraq, Pakistan)

Case study: **Strait of Hormuz** (Tanker War 1980–88 vs 2026 closure).
Compiled 19 October 2026.

## How these were checked (read this first)

- Every article below was found with web searches (extended mode) run on 19 October 2026. Each URL appeared in the search results with the headline shown.
- **Opening the pages directly did not work in this session.** The web-fetch tool and the network proxy blocked every news site (Al Jazeera, Kuwait Times, Tehran Times, Press TV, Mehr, Tasnim, Kayhan, Iran International and others). So the summaries come from the search-result text, not from reading the full articles. **Before quoting a figure in the EPQ, open the article and check it.**
- **Dates:** where the date is in the URL (for example `/2026/10/04/`) it is reliable. Where it is not, the date is marked "approx." and is taken from the search text. Treat those as roughly right only.
- Type for all entries: **news** (or **opinion/editorial** where marked). Most Gulf outlets are state-owned or close to their governments. Iranian outlets are almost all state- or IRGC-linked. Notes on each outlet are below.

## IRAN

**1. "Iran reaffirms Strait of Hormuz closure, says passage must be coordinated"**: *Tehran Times*, Iran, approx. 15–16 Oct 2026
https://www.tehrantimes.com/news/531128/Iran-reaffirms-Strait-of-Hormuz-closure-says-passage-must-be
- **Summary:** Iranian military officials say the strait is closed to any shipping that has not been coordinated with Iran's armed forces. Tehran says it will not reopen the strait until the US meets Iran's seven conditions.
- **National perspective:** Iran presents the closure as lawful self-defence and as leverage over the US blockade.
- **Bias:** State-linked.
- **Supports:** Length of disruption (by October the closure is in its eighth month). Type of threat (control by permit, not a total blockade).

## Things that contradict or complicate the student's plan

1. **Timeline:** The brief says "ceasefire; Iran re-shut the strait June 2026". The sources show a more complex sequence:
   - 8 Apr: two-week ceasefire, brokered by Pakistan.
   - 13 Apr: US blockade of Iranian ports.
   - 17 Apr: Iran declares the strait "completely open".
   - 18 Apr: Iran re-closes it in response to the blockade.
   - About 17–18 Jun: the US–Iran "Islamabad MoU" briefly reopens it.
   - Press TV says Iran closed it again on 20 June; the IRGC and PGSA declared closures on 11 Jun and 12 Jul after hostilities resumed in early July.

   **Use "re-closed in April, briefly reopened under the June MoU, and closed again from late June/July".**
2. **The multinational mission had not deployed** as of October 2026 (Al Jazeera 7 Aug; The National 6 Oct). The UK–France mission and the US "Maritime Freedom Construct" exist side by side. Saudi Arabia announced its own alliance.
3. **The UNSC route was partly blocked:** Resolution 2817 passed (11 Mar), but a later freedom-of-navigation draft was vetoed by Russia and China.
4. **Pipeline figures conflict:**
   - The Saudi East-West line is given as 7 million b/d nameplate, 3.5 million b/d crude in one analysis, and about 2 million b/d actual flow in August.
   - UAE Habshan–Fujairah is given as 1.5–1.8 million b/d.

   Cite ranges.
5. **The status of the strait is itself disputed.** Iran says it is closed; the US says it is open. Use neutral data: IMF PortWatch recorded 2 transits on 11 Oct against a pre-crisis baseline of about 85 a day (cited by straits.live), and Kpler recorded crude at about 74% of pre-war in early October. These two numbers measure different things (ship counts vs barrels).

## How the national narratives differ

Iranian state media (Press TV, Tasnim, Mehr, Kayhan, IRNA, Tehran Times) present the closure as lawful self-defence against "US-Israeli aggression". They describe it through official-sounding bodies such as the Persian Gulf Strait Authority, call ships that pass without permission "violators", and treat reopening as a reward the US can earn by meeting seven conditions. The London-based opposition channel Iran International uses the same facts to show the regime as reckless. The Arab Gulf states agree on the principle but differ according to their geography. The UAE and Saudi Arabia, which own bypass pipelines to Fujairah and Yanbu, stress resilience and engineering. They condemn Iranian "piracy" through state agencies (WAM, SPA) and reject any toll. Kuwait, Qatar and Bahrain have no way around the strait, so their papers focus on budgets, LNG losses and UN law (Resolution 2817, and Kuwait's reminder of Resolution 552 from the 1980s). Qatar and Oman present themselves as bridges, not combatants. Omani papers even float Iran–Oman "service" arrangements modelled on Malacca, which Riyadh and Abu Dhabi oppose. Iraq's press (Rudaw, Shafaq, Iraqi News) is the most transactional: it reports Baghdad negotiating passage with both Washington and Tehran. Pakistan's Dawn tells a story of national mediation and of fuel inflation at home. Overall, the regional coverage shows cooperation **breaking into bilateral deals by flag and route**, rival coalitions and a veto at the UN. This suggests that in 2026 multilateral cooperation existed on paper but did little to shorten or soften the disruption. In the 1980s, by contrast, US-led reflagging and escorts kept oil flowing despite more than 450 attacks.
