## Contradictions and nuances to flag against the plan

1. **Timeline of the Hormuz "re-shut".** The plan says Iran re-shut the strait in **June** 2026. The sources describe several stages: an April re-closure; a US–Iran MoU signed in mid-June (CFR says 14 June, Al Jazeera 17 June); the truce collapsing on **8 July** (CFR); and the IRGC declaring the strait closed "until further notice" on **12 July**. Check the dates and consider rewording to "the June MoU collapsed in July and Iran re-closed the strait".
2. **"Traffic still far down" needs a qualifier.** *Foreign Policy* (1 Oct 2026, item 18) reports that weekly **crude** flows are back **near pre-war levels**, mostly via a US-guarded southern route, while about 3 mb/d of **refined products** are still missing. Al-Monitor/Reuters reports, by contrast, count only about 6–12 **vessels a day**, against 130–140 before the war, with tankers sailing with transponders off. Both can be true, since there are fewer but larger, "dark" crude tankers. Say which measure you use.
3. **Brent peak differs by source.** CFR gives a peak "above $138". Project Syndicate gives "near $120 in April". *Foreign Affairs* says $110 in September after the Saudi pipeline attack. Use one dated data source (EIA or ICE) for your severity measure.
4. **Red Sea: tactical success is not strategic success.** Several US naval analysts (items 1, 30, 31) argue that Prosperity Guardian **won the battles but did not restore traffic**. This supports your hypothesis that joint operations alone do not cut severity much. Use it as a counter-argument to any claim that the coalition "worked".

## A. United States / Western security viewpoint

### 1. Tallis (2025): Red Sea, tactical win and strategic loss
- **Citation:** Tallis, J. (2025) 'How the Biden Administration Won Tactically but Failed Strategically in the Red Sea', *War on the Rocks*, 2 April.
- **URL:** https://warontherocks.com/2025/04/how-the-biden-administration-won-tactically-but-failed-strategically-in-the-red-sea/
- **Type:** analysis/opinion (not peer-reviewed).
- **Summary:** The US Navy won "nearly flawless" engagements defending itself, merchant ships and allied territory against Houthi missiles and drones. Judged against the real goal of restoring commercial traffic through the Red Sea, Operation Prosperity Guardian failed. Few partners joined, and shipping lines made their own cost calculations and kept rerouting via the Cape.
- **Author:** Joshua Tallis, naval analyst [BK: research scientist at CNA]. He also discussed the piece on the WOTR podcast.
- **Bias:** US defence-establishment insider, critical of the Biden administration's strategy, not of the Navy.
- **Factors:** JO (limits), RS; Alt: insurance and shipping-company decisions; Case 2 (Red Sea 2023–26).

### 2. Samaan (2026): the Asian navies that stayed away
- **Citation:** Samaan, J.-L. (2026) 'The Missing Navies: The Hormuz Crisis and the Limits of America's Indo-Pacific Partnerships', *War on the Rocks*, 13 May.
- **URL:** https://warontherocks.com/the-missing-navies-the-hormuz-crisis-and-the-limits-of-americas-indo-pacific-partnerships/
- **Type:** analysis.
- **Summary:** The Hormuz closure is "fundamentally an Asian problem", because most of the oil goes to Asia, yet Asian governments have mostly refused to contribute ships or leadership. Samaan argues that this exposes the weakness of US coalition-building in Asia compared with Europe.
- **Author:** Jean-Loup Samaan [BK: senior research fellow, Middle East Institute, National University of Singapore]. A French scholar based in Singapore, which gives a useful Asia-facing angle.

### 3. Schroden (2025): what Gallipoli teaches about Hormuz
- **Citation:** Schroden, J. (2025) 'A Strait Comparison: Lessons from the Dardanelles for a Strait of Hormuz Closure', *War on the Rocks*, 30 June.
- **URL:** https://warontherocks.com/2025/06/a-strait-comparison-lessons-from-the-dardanelles-for-a-strait-of-hormuz-closure/
- **Type:** analysis, based on his 2011 CNA report (https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA551248.pdf).
- **Summary:** Schroden uses the failed 1915 Allied naval attack on the Dardanelles to think about how mines and shore batteries could close Hormuz. - **Author:** Jonathan Schroden [BK: CNA research programme director].
- **Bias:** US-Navy-funded research tradition.
- **Factors:** JO, RS (mine clearance); Alt: type of threat; Case 1 (both periods; useful for the Tanker War too).
