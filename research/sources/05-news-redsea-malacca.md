## Read this first: how these sources were checked

- **Blocked sites.** The search tool cannot reach nytimes.com, theguardian.com, reuters.com, apnews.com, bbc.co.uk, elpais.com, abc.es, elconfidencial.com, elmundo.es or rtve.es. Each of these domains returned an "access blocked" error. Instead, I used **Reuters and AP stories reprinted by other newspapers** (Boston Globe, Business Live, Michigan Daily, CNBC Africa, Baird Maritime, ABC News US and others), which is a normal and acceptable way to cite a wire story. For Spanish-language coverage I used La Nación (Argentina), Infobae, Forbes Colombia, Prensa Latina, El Universal (Mexico), The Objective and Moncloa.com.
- **No full-page reads.** WebFetch failed with a DNS error on every site I tried, and the shared search budget then ran out. Every entry below was therefore **seen in search results** (headline, URL and snippet text) but **not read in full**. Before you quote anything word for word, open the link and check it.
- **Dates.** If the search result or the URL itself showed the exact date, I give it. If not, the entry says "date not confirmed", with the best available clue in brackets. Never cite a "date not confirmed" item with an exact day until you have checked it.
- **To get the NYT, Guardian, Time, El País or ABC 1967–75 coverage**, use your school or library login for the NYT TimesMachine, the Guardian/Observer archive (ProQuest), the Time Vault, or ABC's free *hemeroteca* (hemeroteca.abc.es). Search 5–7 June 1975 for the reopening and 5–10 June 1967 for the closure.

# CASE STUDY 2: SUEZ CANAL / RED SEA

## Period A: the 1967–75 closure

### 1. Michigan Daily (AP wire), 6 June 1975: Sadat leads convoy to reopen the canal
- **Citation:** Associated Press (1975) 'Sadat reopens Suez Canal' [wire report], *The Michigan Daily*, 6 June. Available at: https://digital.bentley.umich.edu/midaily/mdp.39015071754639/265 (text: https://digital.bentley.umich.edu/midaily/mdp.39015071754639/265/download_text). The exact headline wording is not confirmed, so check the scan.
- **Type:** News (wire report reprinted in a student newspaper archive). Primary source for 1975.
- **Summary:** This is a contemporary agency report on the reopening ceremony. "Leading a convoy of Egyptian ships dwarfed by a big American cruiser, President Anwar Sadat reopened the Suez Canal", sailing aboard the destroyer *October 6* while crowds on the banks cheered.
- **Key figures:** The canal reopened on 5 June 1975, eight years to the day after it closed on 5 June 1967. The US cruiser was USS *Little Rock*.
- **Supports:** Severity: **length of disruption** (8 years). Cooperation: the visible US role points to the **joint operation** behind the clearance.
- **Reliability:** High for facts, because it was written at the time by a major agency. It is a free, public university archive.

### 2. The Spectator, 7 June 1975: "Suez Canal"
- **Citation:** Samuelson, M. (1975) 'Suez Canal', *The Spectator*, 7 June, p. 11. Available at: https://archive.spectator.co.uk/article/7th-june-1975/11/suez-canal
- **Type:** News / opinion (weekly magazine). Primary source for 1975.
- **Summary:** This editorial calls the reopening "one of the more significant anti-climaxes of our time". It argues that the reopening mattered more as an Egyptian political victory than as a shipping event, because the shipping industry's response was lukewarm. It also notes that Sadat at first allowed only Israeli *cargoes*, not Israeli ships.
- **Key figures:** No numbers appeared in the snippet.
- **Supports:** Alternative factors: **supply-chain design / technology**. By 1975 shipping had adapted to the Cape route with supertankers, so the reopening brought only a small benefit.
- **Reliability:** This is opinion with a British, conservative-leaning viewpoint. It is useful as a contemporary judgement, not as data. The archive is free.

### 3. Aramco World, September/October 1975: "Suez: The Reopening"
- **Citation:** Arndt, R. (1975) 'Suez: the reopening', *Aramco World*, 26(5), September/October. Available at: https://archive.aramcoworld.com/issue/197505/suez-the.reopening.htm
- **Type:** Magazine feature (published by the Saudi Aramco oil company).
- **Summary:** This is an eyewitness-style account of the 5 June 1975 ceremony. A seven-ship flotilla passed through ceremonial gates off Port Said and made a five-hour voyage south to Ismailia. The article stresses that clearing the wartime wreckage was dangerous, technically difficult work that got less attention than the diplomacy, and it describes the canal by 1974 as "a stagnant cesspool".
- **Key figures:** 7 ships in the flotilla; 5 hours from Port Said to Ismailia; 8 years closed.
- **Supports:** Cooperation: **joint operations** (the international clearance). Severity: **length of disruption**.
- **Reliability:** Good descriptive detail. The publisher is an oil company with an interest in the canal. Free to read.

### 5. Al Jazeera, 20 November 2019: "Suez: The Yellow Fleet trapped by the 1967 Arab-Israeli War"
- **Citation:** Al Jazeera World (2019) *Suez: the Yellow Fleet trapped by the 1967 Arab-Israeli War* [documentary], Al Jazeera, 20 November. Available at: https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/video/al-jazeera-world/2019/11/20/suez-the-yellow-fleet-trapped-by-the-1967-arab-israeli-war
- **Type:** News-broadcaster documentary.
- **Summary:** The film tells the story of the merchant ships trapped in the Great Bitter Lake from 1967 to 1975. Their crews formed the "Great Bitter Lake Association" (October 1967) and held a "Bitter Lake Olympics" in 1968.
- **Key figures:** 14 ships in the Great Bitter Lake plus 1 in Lake Timsah (15 in all), from 8 countries. Most had to be towed out in 1975 because their engines had seized.
- **Supports:** Severity: **length of disruption**. The human cost is useful for your introduction.
- **Reliability:** A reputable broadcaster, but it is a retrospective documentary. Al Jazeera is Qatari state-funded.

## Contradictions with the student's plan, and things to check

1. **The Malacca "present-day surge" has already reversed.** Your plan describes the present as a "2025 surge in robberies". That is true for 2025 (108 incidents, the highest since 2007). But ReCAAP data for 2026 shows a fall of 64–74% by mid-2026, which ReCAAP credits to littoral-state enforcement and industry measures (items 54–55).
