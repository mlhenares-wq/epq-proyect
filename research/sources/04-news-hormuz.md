### Things that change or contradict the student's plan
1. **"Iran re-shut the strait in June 2026" is correct, but it is only part of the story.** The sequence was:
   - 17 June: the Islamabad memorandum reopened the strait on 18 June.
   - 21 June (a Sunday): Iran said it had shut the strait again (Reuters, 22 June).
   - 7–8 July: the ceasefire broke down over shipping.
   - 14 July: the US resumed its blockade of Iranian ports.
   - About 17 October: the IRGC Navy declared the strait closed once more.
   
   The plan should show these several closures, not a single re-closure.
2. **Transit numbers disagree sharply.**
   - IMF PortWatch counted 2 transits on 11 October, against about 85 a day before the crisis.
   - Merca2 (11 Oct) claimed traffic was at "about 80%" of pre-war levels. That looks wrong, or it may be measuring something else.
   - Iran, US CENTCOM, Kpler and Windward all give different daily counts.
   
   Always say which tracker a number comes from.
3. **The UK–France mission had not clearly started as of 6 October 2026.** Its planning is well documented. Reports differ on its size: 15, about 30 or up to 40 nations.
4. **Spain's exposure is disputed.**
   - Deutsche Bank: less than 10% of Spain's oil imports come from the Middle East.
   - Moncloa.com: about a quarter of Spain's crude imports use the Hormuz route.

## Period 1: The Tanker War, 1980–88 (chronological)

**1. UPI (1984) 'The Iran-Iraq war in the Persian Gulf has cost...', *UPI Archives*, 9 March.**
URL: https://www.upi.com/Archives/1984/03/09/The-Iran-Iraq-war-in-the-Persian-Gulf-has-cost/3441276045960/
- **Summary:** An early wire report on what the shipping war was costing marine insurers, based on an estimate from Lloyd's Shipping Information Services.
- **Key figure:** more than **$500 million** in losses to international marine insurance markets since September 1980.
- **Supports:** the likelihood measure, insurance (the cost of risk); length of disruption (the war on shipping was already 3+ years old).

**2. Washington Post (1987) 'Kuwait rejects U.S. offer of Gulf tanker escorts', *The Washington Post*, 28 March.**
URL: https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1987/03/28/kuwait-rejects-us-offer-of-gulf-tanker-escorts/2d0b325d-845a-43f0-8d7c-3bd110d6186c/
- **Summary:** Kuwait at first turned down a US offer of naval escorts, to avoid a bigger superpower presence in the Gulf. This came after Washington's 7 March offer to reflag 11 Kuwaiti tankers. The story shows that cooperation was negotiated and hesitant at the start.
- **Supports:** cooperation factor, formal frameworks; response speed.

**3. UPI (1987) 'The Pentagon raised the toll today to 37 sailors...', *UPI Archives*, 19 May.**
URL: https://www.upi.com/Archives/1987/05/19/The-Pentagon-raised-the-toll-today-to-37-sailors/3451548395200/
- **Summary:** Reports the death toll from the Iraqi Exocet attack on the frigate USS Stark on 17 May 1987. A related UPI story the same day (https://www.upi.com/Archives/1987/05/19/The-USS-Starks-sophisticated-radar-system-failed-to-pick/3677548395200/) says the ship's radar failed to pick up the threat.
- **Key figures:** **37** US sailors killed; two Exocet missiles fired from an Iraqi Mirage F-1. The US accepted Saddam Hussein's apology.
- **Supports:** the likelihood measure, incident numbers; context, because this was the attack that led directly to US escorts.

**4. Christian Science Monitor (1987) 'GULF ATTACK. Stark's captain says Iraqi missile spotted "seconds" before hit', *CSM*, 21 May.**
URL: https://www.csmonitor.com/1987/0521/ocrew.html
- **Summary:** Captain Glenn Brindel said his crew detected the incoming missile only seconds before impact. The paper describes what was termed an accidental attack by an Iraqi jet.
- **Supports:** the technology factor (detection gaps); information sharing (Iraq and the US were not coordinating).

**5. Washington Post (1987) 'Panel faults officers of USS Stark', *The Washington Post*, 14 June.**
URL: https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1987/06/14/panel-faults-officers-of-uss-stark/b199a8e6-8552-4494-9089-167e78a9605b/
- **Summary:** A preliminary report by the House Armed Services Committee said the Stark's officers reacted too late to the approaching Iraqi aircraft.
- **Supports:** response speed; technology.

**6. UPI (1987) 'Nunn: Reflagging Kuwaiti tankers risky, unpredictable', *UPI Archives*, 29 June.**
URL: https://www.upi.com/Archives/1987/06/29/Nunn-Reflagging-Kuwaiti-tankers-risky-unpredictable/9769551937600/
- **Summary:** Senator Sam Nunn, chairman of the Senate Armed Services Committee, warned that the reflagging plan was risky. This is useful for showing that US policy at the time was debated, not agreed by everyone.
- **Supports:** formal frameworks (a unilateral US–Kuwait deal, not a multilateral one).

**7. UPI (1987) 'U.S. warships escorted two reflagged Kuwaiti tankers up the...', *UPI Archives*, 23 July.**
URL: https://www.upi.com/Archives/1987/07/23/US-warships-escorted-two-reflagged-Kuwaiti-tankers-up-the/9564554011200/
- **Summary:** Reports the first Operation Earnest Will convoy entering the Gulf, with US warships escorting the reflagged tankers. Earnest Will ran from 24 July 1987 to 26 September 1988 and became the largest naval convoy operation since WWII.
- **Supports:** cooperation factor, joint operations / escorts.

**8. UPI (1987) 'The supertanker Bridgeton hit an underwater mine Friday morning...', *UPI Archives*, 24 July.**
URL: https://www.upi.com/Archives/1987/07/24/The-supertanker-Bridgeton-hit-an-underwater-mine-Friday-morning/4397554097600/
- **Summary:** The reflagged supertanker Bridgeton struck a mine during the very first convoy. The Pentagon believed it was a moored mine laid before the convoy, and the Navy pointed to Iran. Nobody was hurt and the ship reached Kuwait under its own power.
- **Key figure:** about a **30 × 10 ft hole** in the hull, as found by divers (UPI).
- **Supports:** incident numbers; type of threat (mines get past escorts); the limits of escort-only cooperation.

**9. Christian Science Monitor (1987) 'Gulf fleet navigates sea of problems. US warships more vulnerable than oil tankers to Iranian mines', *CSM*, 29 July.**
URL: https://www.csmonitor.com/1987/0729/onavy.html
- **Summary:** Explains that US warships were more at risk from mines than the large tankers they escorted. The US had little mine-clearing capability in the area, and the damage to Bridgeton forced the convoy to slow to below 9 knots.
- **Supports:** technology; type of threat; why European minesweepers were then needed.

**10. Washington Post (1987) 'Escorted ships slip into Persian Gulf', *The Washington Post*, 9 August.**
URL: https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1987/08/09/escorted-ships-slip-into-persian-gulf/af70e933-e9d7-4e36-8e37-dd35937dd6da/
- **Summary:** A later US-escorted convoy of reflagged Kuwaiti tankers passed through the Strait of Hormuz into the Gulf.
- **Supports:** joint operations; trade continuity (traffic kept moving despite the threat).

**11. UPI (1987) 'Britain, France sending mine sweepers to Persian, Oman gulfs', *UPI Archives*, 11 August.**
URL: https://www.upi.com/Archives/1987/08/11/Britain-France-sending-mine-sweepers-to-Persian-Oman-gulfs/5251555652800/
- **Summary:** London and Paris announced minesweeper deployments after the Bridgeton mining. Belgium, the Netherlands and Italy later joined. The European effort was coordinated loosely through the Western European Union, not NATO (MERIP, Dec 1988, https://merip.org/1988/11/nato-goes-to-the-persian-gulf/ ; this is an analysis magazine, not a newspaper). Britain sent 4 minesweepers plus a support ship to reinforce its Armilla Patrol.
- **Supports:** multilateral cooperation, joint operations (the closest 1987 parallel to the 2026 UK–France mission).

**12. UPI (1987) 'Once obscure port caught up in Iran-Iraq war', *UPI Archives*, 17 August.**
URL: https://www.upi.com/Archives/1987/08/17/Once-obscure-port-caught-up-in-Iran-Iraq-war/3779556171200/
- **Summary:** Lloyd's of London had treated Fujairah (UAE, outside the strait) as safe. It began charging war-risk premiums there after mines hit shipping nearby, so the zone of risk was spreading.
- **Supports:** the likelihood measure, war-risk insurance premiums. The search snippet gave no per-voyage rate.

**13. UPI (1987) 'Missile strikes U.S. flagged ship; 18 injured', *UPI Archives*, 16 October.** (Same event: Washington Post, 'Iran hits U.S.-owned tanker', 16 Oct 1987, https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1987/10/16/iran-hits-us-owned-tanker/bf2e5e5c-21c4-4ae3-9da3-b68002bfc58e/)
