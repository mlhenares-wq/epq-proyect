# 09 – Shipping and energy trade press, 2023–2026 (Hormuz, Red Sea/Suez, Malacca)

Compiled 19 October 2026 for Manuela López-Henares's EPQ.

## How these sources were checked (please read first)

- **Every article below appeared in live web-search results** (headline + URL + text snippet) during this session. None is invented.
- **I could not open the full pages.** Lloyd's List, TradeWinds, gCaptain, Hellenic Shipping News, Insurance Journal, Argus, OilPrice and S&P Global were all blocked from this research environment. The figures come from search snippets of each article, so **open each link yourself before quoting**. Lloyd's List, TradeWinds, Insurance Day and S&P are partly or fully **paywalled**.
- **Dates.** "Exact" means the date is printed in the URL (Insurance Journal, S&P Global, Rigzone, LMA) or appeared in the article text. "Approx." means I worked it out from the events the article describes, so check it.
- The search budget for this session ran out before I could confirm a few more dates (OilPrice $510k VLCC story, Argus "Hormuz tanker traffic unchanged after US-Iran deal"). Those two are listed under "Seen but not verified" at the end and not counted.
- **Reliability in general:** trade press is written for the industry and is usually accurate on rates and premiums, because traders and brokers rely on it. But many figures are *quotes from brokers* (Marsh, Howden) or *index assessments* (Baltic Exchange TD3C, Drewry WCI), not prices actually paid. AIS-based transit counts **undercount "dark" transits**, where ships switch their transponders off (see H12 and H25).

## A. Strait of Hormuz (2026 crisis)

### H1. LMA Joint War Committee – JWLA-033 (3 March 2026). Primary source / insurance circular
- **Citation:** Lloyd's Market Association (2026) *JWLA-033 Joint War Committee Circular*, 3 March. https://lmalloyds.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/JWLA-033_Iran.pdf
- **Date:** 3 March 2026 (exact).
- **Summary:** The London market's Joint War Committee published a new, much larger "listed area" covering the Persian/Arabian Gulf, the Gulf of Oman, part of the Indian Ocean, the Gulf of Aden and the southern Red Sea. Ships entering a listed area must tell their war insurers and usually pay an extra "additional premium" (AP).
- **Key figures:** Gard (H3) says the circular added five countries: Bahrain, Djibouti, Kuwait, Oman and Qatar.
- **Supports:** Likelihood → insurance premiums; Cooperation → information sharing and speed of response by the insurance market.

### H2. Lloyd's List – "Crude tanker rates in unchartered territory; VLCC index tops $420K"
- **URL:** https://www.lloydslist.com/LL1156492/Crude-tanker-rates-in-unchartered-territory-VLCC-index-tops-420K
- **Date:** approx. 2–3 March 2026. It reports a record set on "Monday", which CNBC dates to 2 March.
- **Summary:** The Baltic Exchange's Middle East Gulf–China supertanker index (TD3C) hit an all-time record as Iranian attacks and insurers pulling war cover effectively closed the strait. The global average for VLCCs was the highest since at least 2008.
- **Key figures:** TD3C $423,736/day. Global average VLCC index $280,941/day.
- **Supports:** Likelihood → freight rates; Severity → trade reduction.

### H6. Lloyd's List – "Gulf war risk premiums topping double-digit millions of dollars per trip"
- **URL:** https://www.lloydslist.com/LL1156586/Gulf-war-risk-premiums-topping-double-digit-millions-of-dollars-per-trip
- **Date:** approx. 11–12 March 2026 (the text mentions "Wednesday, March 11").
- **Summary:** War cover for high-risk Gulf voyages was being quoted at 7.5% of hull value, and brokers expected 10% or more. For a supertanker, that means a premium of over $10 million for one trip. Seven-day Gulf cover was up tenfold on pre-war levels.
- **Key figures:** Pre-war APs of 0.15–0.25% of hull value. Quotes of 7.5% rising to 10%+. Marsh average ratings of 0.8–1.5%. The 1980s Tanker War rate was about 5%, a useful comparison for the student's case study.
- **Supports:** Likelihood → insurance premiums; useful for comparing the Tanker War with 2026.

## B. Red Sea / Suez Canal / Bab el-Mandeb (2023–2026)

### R1. gCaptain – "Maersk Halts Red Sea Voyages 'Until Further Notice'"
- **URL:** https://gcaptain.com/maersk-pausing-red-sea-transits-until-further-notice/
- **Date:** approx. 2–5 January 2024 (exact date not visible). The events it covers run from 14 December 2023 to early January 2024.
- **Summary:** Maersk first paused Red Sea transits on 15 December 2023, the day after a missile near-miss on *Maersk Gibraltar*. - **Key figures:** Dates: 14 Dec, 15 Dec and 31 Dec 2023.
- **Supports:** Rerouting via the Cape; Cooperation → Operation Prosperity Guardian did *not* restore confidence, which matters for the "how far does cooperation help" argument.

## Things that matter for the student's plan

1. **Hormuz numbers depend on who is counting.** In October 2026, US-escorted counts (~30/day) and JMIC/commercial AIS counts (single digits) differ hugely (H12, H24, H25). Say which one you use.
2. **Premiums are quotes, not a tariff.** Figures move from 0.15–0.25% before the war to 7.5–10% in March, ~5% on 19 March, 3–4% after the June deal, mid-single digits in early July, and 7.5–10% in late July. One unverified website claims ~1.5% in October 2026, which I could not confirm. Do not quote a single "Hormuz premium".
3. **Red Sea: the Cape route stayed in use after attacks stopped** (R10). Transits were still −60% 100 days after the last attack. This supports the argument that military cooperation alone does not restore trade quickly.
4. **Malacca: the 2025 surge is Singapore Strait petty theft** (M5). It was followed by a 74% fall in H1 2026 credited to enforcement (M9). That is the clearest "cooperation works" case in this list, but it is low-level crime, not a state or militia threat, so the threat types are not like for like.
5. **Joint military action can raise premiums in the short term.** US/UK strikes in January 2024 tripled Red Sea rates (R2).
6. Brief check: the 20 July 2026 Houthi blockade of Saudi Arabia is confirmed (R11). The sources describe the June 2026 Hormuz events as a preliminary US–Iran deal on 18 June, followed by the ceasefire collapsing in early July. Check this against the brief's wording, "Iran re-shut the strait June 2026".
