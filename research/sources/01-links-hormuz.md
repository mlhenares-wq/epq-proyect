## 1. BIS Working Paper 1378: Maritime chokepoints and the global economy

- **URL:** https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1378-maritime-chokepoints-and-the-global-economy-evidence-strait-hormuz
- **Status:** Confirmed in search results (BIS web page, BIS PDF, and RePEc listing `RePEc:bis:biswps:1378`).
- **Citation:** Kharroubi, E. (2026) *Maritime chokepoints and the global economy: evidence from the Strait of Hormuz*. BIS Working Papers No. 1378. Basel: Bank for International Settlements. Available at: [URL] (Accessed: 19 October 2026).
- **Type:** Report / working paper (not peer-reviewed, but from a central-bank research institution).
- **Summary:** The paper uses real-time ship-traffic data to measure unusual drops in traffic at chokepoints, after adjusting for normal patterns and ship size. It uses oil-market announcements to separate supply-driven drops from demand-driven ones. It finds that disruption at Hormuz raises energy and fertiliser prices, especially when the drop is supply-driven. Global industrial production then weakens and consumer prices rise. These effects appear after about 4–6 months and can last up to a year. Credit conditions also tighten, with corporate bond spreads widening, especially for high-yield borrowers.
- **Key points:** Hormuz disruption acts as a "stagflationary" force. Of all the chokepoints studied, Hormuz gives the clearest and most consistent signal of global supply stress (as reported by *Central Banking*). Search results date the paper to 17 September 2026.
- **Case study / measures:** Hormuz. Severity: rise in the general price level, trade reduction. Also useful as a comparison across chokepoints (alternative factors).
- **Reliability:** High-quality institution, free to read. Working papers carry a disclaimer that the views are the author's own. Not peer-reviewed.
- **Plan accuracy:** Fits the topic well. One of the strongest sources on severity.
