## 22. Wikipedia: 1974 Suez Canal Clearance Operation

- **Citation:** Wikipedia (n.d.) *1974 Suez Canal Clearance Operation*. Available at: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Suez_Canal_Clearance_Operation (Accessed: 19 October 2026).
- **Type:** Encyclopedia (tertiary).
- **Works?** Indexed under the expected title. Not fetched directly (proxy block).
- **Summary:** After the 1973 Yom Kippur War, an agreement in October 1973 led to an international effort to reopen the canal, which had been shut since June 1967. The US-led effort had three parts: **Nimbus Star** (minesweeping by US, UK and French naval units), **Nimbus Moon** (training Egyptian forces to clear explosives) and **Nimrod Spar** (removing wrecks). The clearance mission began on 11 April 1974. The canal reopened on **5 June 1975**.
- **Key facts (from the search results, US Navy and other sources):** More than 1,500 Egyptian personnel were trained. More than 200 tons of unexploded ordnance and 686,000 land and anti-personnel mines were removed. Ten sunken ships were raised. Mine and ordnance clearance was finished by 15–20 December 1974 (sources differ by a few days). The UK's part was called Operation Rheostat (HMS Abdiel, Bossington, Maxton, Wilton). British work in the canal began on 1 May 1974.
- **Case study / factor:** Suez 1967–75. Joint operations, response speed, length of disruption.
- **Reliability:** Fine for background. Cite the primary sources it points to (links 23 and 27) in the essay.

Use February (the publisher's own page).

## 24. RePEc/IDEAS: Feyrer, SED 2011 Meeting Paper 1438

- **Citation:** Feyrer, J. (2011) 'Distance, Trade, and Income – The 1967 to 1975 Closing of the Suez Canal as a Natural Experiment', *2011 Meeting Papers* 1438, Society for Economic Dynamics. Available at: https://ideas.repec.org/p/red/sed011/1438.html (Accessed: 19 October 2026).
- **Type:** Working paper. The final version is peer-reviewed (see below).
- **Works?** Indexed under the expected title. Not fetched directly.
- **Summary:** The 1967–75 closure works as a "natural experiment". It suddenly made sea trips longer for some country pairs but not others, which lets Feyrer measure how distance affects trade and how trade affects income. He finds that the effect of distance on trade is about **half** the size that standard cross-country estimates suggest. He also finds that trade has a significant effect on income, mainly through trade in goods (not technology transfer or foreign investment).
- **Published version (plan fact CONFIRMED):** Feyrer, J. (2021) 'Distance, trade, and income – The 1967 to 1975 closing of the Suez canal as a natural experiment', *Journal of Development Economics*, 153, 102708. **DOI: 10.1016/j.jdeveco.2021.102708**. It started as NBER Working Paper 15557 (December 2009): https://www.nber.org/papers/w15557
- **Case study / factor:** Suez 1967–75. Severity: trade reduction and income.
- **Reliability:** High. Cite the 2021 journal article (peer-reviewed), not the SED version.

## 27. DTIC AD-A010 261: CTF 65 Final Report (1975)

- **Citation:** Commander Task Force Sixty-Five (1975) *CTF Sixty-Five Final Report: Suez Canal Clearance Operations Nimbus Star, Nimbus Moon, Nimrod Spar, 11 April – 15 December 1974*. Naval Inshore Warfare Command, Atlantic. DTIC AD-A010 261. Available at: https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA010261.pdf (Accessed: 19 October 2026).
- **Type:** Primary (official military report).
- **Works?** Indexed. Search shows the front matter and contents page. Not fetched directly.
- **Summary:** The official final report of US Task Force 65 on the 1974 clearance. It covers all three operations, lessons learned, logistics and methods. The task force chose **air mine countermeasures** (helicopter sweeping) as its main search method, partly because of reports that an Israeli aircraft had dropped a Mk 36 Destructor mine. Dated May 1975. It gives 15 December 1974 as the end of operations (GlobalSecurity says 20 December).
- **Case study / factor:** Suez 1967–75. Joint operations, response speed, technology.
- **Reliability:** Authoritative primary source, but written from the US point of view. It may say less about the UK, French and Egyptian roles.
