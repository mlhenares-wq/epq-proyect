## Debate 1. Malacca: did patrols cause the 2005 drop, or did the tsunami and the Aceh peace deal?

### 1a. Al Jazeera (2005) "Piracy disappears in tsunami's wake"
- **Citation:** Al Jazeera (2005) 'Piracy disappears in tsunami's wake', *Al Jazeera*, 1 February. Available at: https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2005/2/1/piracy-disappears-in-tsunamis-wake (Accessed: 19 October 2026).
- **Parallel report:** NBC News (2005) 'Piracy disappears after tsunami'. Available at: https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna6937248
- **Type:** News (contemporary; it quotes the International Maritime Bureau, IMB).
- **Summary:** An IMB official, Noel Choong, reported that no attacks had been recorded in the Malacca Strait since the 26 December 2004 tsunami. He suggested that pirates' boats and weapons had been destroyed, or the pirates themselves killed, and that the large aid operation in Sumatra also played a part.
- **Case study / factor:** Malacca, period 1. It is evidence for an *alternative factor* (a natural disaster, and "type of threat") rather than cooperation.
- **Key quote (from the search snippet):** "The tsunami seems to have really succeeded where the joint patrols could not."
- **Reliability:** Reputable news, quoting the main industry watchdog. It was written only weeks after the event, so it is a first impression, not an analysis.

### 1b. Gulf News (2005/06) "Piracy declines sharply in Malacca Straits" and Malaysiakini, "Less piracy in Malacca Straits"
- **Citation:** Gulf News (n.d., c.2005–06) 'Piracy declines sharply in Malacca Straits', *Gulf News*. Available at: https://gulfnews.com/business/piracy-declines-sharply-in-malacca-straits-1.224149 ; Malaysiakini (n.d.) 'Less piracy in Malacca Straits'. Available at: https://www.malaysiakini.com/news/43847 (Accessed: 19 October 2026).
- **Type:** News.
- **Summary:** The IMB director, Pottengal Mukundan, linked the drop in late 2005 to more patrols by Indonesia on its side of the strait (Operation Gurita 2005). He warned that the problem returns "when it goes off the agenda". Another report from the same search links the fall off Belawan to the 2005 Helsinki peace deal between Jakarta and GAM (the Free Aceh Movement), since some GAM elements were suspected of piracy.
- **Case study / factor:** Malacca. *Enforcement of laws* (but national Indonesian patrols, not multilateral ones), plus *connected crises* (the Aceh conflict).
- **Reliability:** Only search snippets were seen, and the exact dates were not confirmed. The search tool also noted that attack counts differ between reports (for example 38→12 in one, 25→10 in another), so take your figures from the IMB annual report itself.

### 1c. Singapore Ministry of Defence (2016) "Malacca Straits Patrol: Member States commemorate 10 years of cooperation" (the opposing view)
- **Citation:** MINDEF Singapore (2016) 'Malacca Straits Patrol: Member States Commemorate 10 Years of Cooperation', news release, 21 April. Available at: https://www.mindef.gov.sg/news-and-events/latest-releases/2016apr21-news-releases-01782/ (Accessed: 19 October 2026).
- **Supporting:** Business Insurance (2006) 'Strait of Malacca off high-risk list'. Available at: https://www.businessinsurance.com/strait-of-malacca-off-high-risk-list/
- **Type:** Primary (official government release) plus trade news.
- **Summary:** Singapore credits the Malacca Straits Patrol with getting Lloyd's Joint War Committee to drop the strait's "war-risk area" listing in August 2006. Business Insurance reports that the committee acted after its security consultant reviewed the littoral states' anti-piracy efforts, including seven joint air patrols a week ("Eyes in the Sky").
- **Case study / factor:** Malacca. *Joint operations*, with *insurance premiums* as the likelihood measure.
- **Reliability:** The MINDEF release is self-promoting by nature, since governments credit their own policy. Business Insurance is an independent trade source, which makes it stronger evidence.

**How to use Debate 1:** This is a **causation problem**. Several things happened at once in 2004–06: the tsunami (Dec 2004), MALSINDO patrols (July 2004), Eyes in the Sky (Sept 2005), the Aceh peace deal (Aug 2005) and ReCAAP (2006). You cannot separate their effects from incident numbers alone. One good move is **timing**: the tsunami explains the early-2005 drop, while patrols and peace explain why incidents *stayed* low. You can then argue that cooperation mattered most for **likelihood as insurers saw it** (the war-risk listing removed in 2006), even if natural and political factors drove the first fall. The 2025 surge (Debate 2) also supports the "it returns when attention lapses" point.

## Debate 2. Malacca: is piracy an economic problem that patrols cannot fix? (the 2025 surge)

### 2a. CIMSEC, "It's the Economy: Exploring Indonesia's Piracy Problem"
- **Citation:** CIMSEC (n.d., c.2014) 'It's the Economy: Exploring Indonesia's Piracy Problem', *Center for International Maritime Security*. Available at: https://cimsec.org/economy-stupid-exploring-indonesias-piracy-problem/ (Accessed: 19 October 2026).
- **Type:** Analysis / think-tank blog (not peer-reviewed).
- **Summary:** It argues that piracy off Indonesia is "inherently an economic problem" and that force alone treats only the symptoms. It notes coastal poverty, and Indonesian incidents rising from 15 in 2009 to 106 in 2013, which happened *while* the joint patrols were running.
- **Case study / factor:** Malacca. *Alternative factor*: regional economic conditions.
- **Reliability:** A well-known naval-affairs forum. The author's name and date were not seen in the snippet, so check them.

### 2b. Maritime Executive (2025) "ReCAAP sounds alarm about tripling of robberies in Singapore Strait in 2025"
- **Citation:** The Maritime Executive (2025) 'ReCAAP sounds alarm about tripling of robberies in Singapore Strait in 2025'. Available at: https://maritime-executive.com/article/recaap-sounds-alarm-about-tripling-of-robberies-in-singapore-strait-in-2025 (Accessed: 19 October 2026).
- **Also:** Seatrade Maritime, 'Ships urged to be vigilant in Singapore Strait as robberies surge'. Available at: https://www.seatrade-maritime.com/piracy/ships-urged-to-be-vigilant-in-singapore-strait-as-robberies-surge
- **Type:** Trade news based on ReCAAP ISC data.
- **Summary:** It reports 50 Singapore Strait incidents by early May 2025, against 14 in the same period of 2024. In the first half of 2025, 79 of the 80 incidents in the Malacca and Singapore Straits were in the Singapore Strait. Most were low-level thefts in the eastbound lane near Phillip Channel, but some robbers now carry knives or possibly guns. ReCAAP can only issue alerts, and it urges the littoral states to patrol more.
- **Case study / factor:** Malacca, period 2. *Information sharing* works (we know about the incidents) but *enforcement* lags.
- **Reliability:** Good trade press that reports ReCAAP figures accurately. Check the numbers against ReCAAP's own half-year report.

**How to use Debate 2:** Use it to show that cooperation **lowers the severity of incidents but not their number**. Today's incidents are small thefts, not hijackings of tankers as in the 1990s–2000s. So cooperation may have changed the *type of threat* without removing its *root cause* (poverty). This fits your "type of threat" and "regional economic growth" alternative factors. It also exposes a design weakness: ReCAAP shares information but has no power to enforce.

## Debate 3. Sovereignty: did coastal states resist outside navies, and did that limit cooperation?

### 3a. Pacific Forum (2004) PacNet 29 "U.S. RMSI contravenes UN Convention on the Law of the Sea"
- **Citation:** Pacific Forum CSIS (2004) 'PacNet 29: U.S. RMSI contravenes UN Convention on the Law of the Sea'. Related PacNet PDF: https://pacforum.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/pac0429.pdf
- **Type:** Policy commentary.
- **Summary:** This is a Malaysian-perspective argument that the US Regional Maritime Security Initiative (RMSI) was illegal because the waters of the strait are the coastal states' territorial waters, and foreign military activity there needs their consent. The PDF also describes the June 2004 Indonesia–Malaysia joint-patrol agreement as a response to RMSI.
- **Case study / factor:** Malacca. *Formal frameworks* and their political limits.
- **Reliability:** A short opinion piece with a clear national viewpoint. It is useful as evidence of *attitudes* at the time.

### 3b. RSIS (2004) "Operationalising the Regional Maritime Security Initiative"
- **Citation:** RSIS / IDSS (2004) 'Operationalising the Regional Maritime Security Initiative', *IDSS Commentaries*, 27 May. Available at: https://rsis.edu.sg/rsis-publication/idss/614-operationalising-the-regional/ (Accessed: 19 October 2026).
- **Type:** Think-tank commentary (Singapore).
- **Summary:** It explains that the RMSI followed Admiral Fargo's 31 March 2004 testimony about putting special forces on high-speed boats in the strait. It notes that patrols by an outside power were seen in the region as contrary to transit-passage rules.
- **Case study / factor:** Malacca. *Joint operations* and who leads them.
- **Reliability:** A respected regional institute. Singapore was the littoral state most open to US involvement, which is worth noting as a possible bias.

### 3c. Febrica, S. (2014) "Securing the Sulu-Sulawesi Seas from maritime terrorism"
- **Citation:** Febrica, S. (2014) 'Securing the Sulu-Sulawesi Seas from maritime terrorism: a troublesome cooperation?', *Perspectives on Terrorism*. University of Strathclyde repository. Available at: https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/76406/1/Febrica_POT_2014_Securing_the_Sulu_Sulawesi_sea_from_maratime.pdf (Accessed: 19 October 2026).
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed journal article (open-access repository copy).
- **Summary:** It argues that Indonesia's rejection was not only about sovereignty. It was also about an institution that seemed "overly militaristic" and Jakarta having no say in designing it. An Indonesian official said direct US Marine patrols were never actually offered.
- **Case study / factor:** Malacca. *Formal frameworks*: design and ownership matter.
- **Reliability:** Peer-reviewed, and the strongest academic source in this debate. Check the journal name and full subtitle on the PDF's first page.

**How to use Debate 3:** This is a strong nuance for your conclusion. **Cooperation works best when coastal states own it.** Outside pressure (RMSI) *triggered* local cooperation (MALSINDO). So "multilateral" in Malacca meant *regional*, not Western-led. Compare this with the Red Sea and Hormuz, where cooperation was led from outside (US/EU) and the coastal actor (the Houthis or Iran) was the threat itself.
